### Research Brief: Regulation of the Japanese Trading Card Market

**1. Verification Status: TRUE**
The Japanese government, specifically through a newly established task force of the ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP), is actively studying and considering regulatory measures for the trading card market. This move follows reports of the market's rapid expansion and associated risks, including counterfeiting, money laundering, and aggressive scalping practices.

**2. Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **Market Size:** The domestic trading card market in Japan reached approximately **¥338.4 billion (approx. US$2.3 billion)** in fiscal year 2025.
*   **Growth:** The market has seen a **90% expansion** over the past four years.
*   **Task Force:** An LDP-led working group held its inaugural meeting on July 23, 2026, to discuss potential policies with industry representatives and the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI).
*   **Corporate Action:** The Pokémon Company has separately announced plans to implement mandatory identity verification using Japan’s **"My Number Card"** system for online purchases and tournament entry, effective August 2026, to combat scalping.

**3. Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Previous Growth:** The market has grown every year since 2017. Data indicates the market value was significantly lower in previous fiscal years (e.g., ~¥302.4 billion in FY2024, with consistent growth from ~¥122.2 billion in FY2020).
*   **Regulatory Precedent:** Lawmakers have explicitly cited Japan's past handling of the cryptocurrency market as a cautionary tale, aiming to avoid overly restrictive regulations that might drive businesses to relocate to the U.S. or other jurisdictions.

**4. Macro/Geopolitical Context**
*   **Shift in Asset Class:** Trading cards, once viewed as children's toys, are now treated as "alternative investment assets."
*   **Sovereignty/Control:** There is a stated concern that while iconic franchises like Pokémon and Yu-Gi-Oh! originated in Japan, the valuation of rare cards is often heavily influenced by U.S.-based grading agencies (e.g., PSA), which the Japanese government views as a potential economic vulnerability.
*   **Crime Prevention:** High-value cards are increasingly being linked to organized crime, money laundering, and violent retail burglaries.

**5. Most Affected Party & Impact**
*   **Losers:** 
    *   **Scalpers:** The primary target of the proposed regulations and new ID requirements.
    *   **Gray Market Resellers:** Individuals or entities using high-value cards to move or launder money.
*   **Winners/Beneficiaries:**
    *   **Genuine Collectors/Players:** Should theoretically gain better access to retail-priced products as scalping is suppressed.
    *   **The Pokémon Company/IP Holders:** Benefit from a more stable, "legitimate" market environment that protects brand integrity.
*   **Financial Flow:** The government is looking to shift the flow of capital away from opaque, unregulated "gray" transactions toward transparent, compliant channels that can be tracked, taxed, and monitored.

**6. Secondary/Hidden Effects**
*   **Authentication Industry Growth:** Increased regulation will likely drive demand for, and professionalization of, domestic Japanese authentication and grading services to compete with U.S. firms.
*   **Market Liquidity:** Stricter KYC (Know Your Customer) requirements could lead to a short-term contraction in liquidity, as anonymous high-value trades become significantly more difficult.
*   **"Dystopian" Precedent:** The requirement of government ID (My Number Card) for purchasing hobby goods is seen by some as a significant escalation in state oversight of private consumer behavior.
