**True.** Reports confirm that international shipping firms, most notably the **Sinokor Group**, have been offering seafarers significant financial bonuses—as high as six months of extra salary—to transit the Strait of Hormuz. ### **2. Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **The Bonus:** Sinokor Group, the world's largest owner of supertankers, is offering an **additional six months of salary** for a single round-trip voyage through the Strait of Hormuz (a journey estimated to take approximately one month).
*   **Base Pay Context:** On oil tankers, captains earn up to $15,000/month, while junior sailors (ratings) earn roughly $1,500/month. The six-month bonus effectively represents a massive, temporary salary multiplier for the duration of the high-risk voyage.
*   **Operational Status:** The Strait is experiencing near-total operational paralysis. Daily transits have plummeted to single-digit figures (e.g., only 3–7 vessels per day recently).
*   **Casualties:** Since the conflict began on February 28, 2026, at least **59 commercial ships** have been attacked and **17 seafarers have lost their lives**.

### **3. Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Normal Traffic:** Prior to the conflict, the Strait of Hormuz was a primary artery for global energy, handling roughly **25% of the world's seaborne oil trade** and **20% of global LNG**.
*   **Previous Incentives:** While industry-standard "war risk" bonuses exist, the current Sinokor offer is described as exceptionally generous compared to other firms, which are reportedly offering bonuses equivalent to roughly 60 days of pay for a 30-day contract.
*   **Traffic Trends:** Daily transits have dropped from a peak of nearly 5,000 vessels in early July to single digits by July 20, 2026.

### **4. Macro/Geopolitical Context**
The bonuses are a direct symptom of the **2026 Iran War**, which began in late February. The U.S. has been conducting sustained military strikes (11 consecutive nights as of July 22, 2026) against Iranian assets, while Iran has responded by targeting commercial shipping and declaring the strait "closed" to allies of the U.S. and Israel. The shipping industry is effectively functioning as a "war zone" logistics provider, with companies like Sinokor aggressively positioning their fleets to capture massive premiums on the limited oil that still moves through the region.

### **5. Most Affected Party & Impact**
*   **Seafarers:** They face the most immediate risk—death or injury—for financial gain. While some are enticed by the "life-changing" nature of the bonuses, others are refusing the work, leading to crew shortages.
*   **Shipping Companies (e.g., Sinokor):** They stand to make massive windfalls. Reports suggest that just three tankers operating continuously since April could generate $60 million to $120 million in profit.
*   **Global Energy Markets:** Consumers and economies face higher prices and supply volatility as the world’s most vital energy chokepoint becomes increasingly unreliable.

### **6. Secondary/Hidden Effects**

*   **Insurance/Reinsurance Crisis:** The event is testing the limits of the global reinsurance market, as insurers grapple with multi-line events (hull, liability, cargo) in a region where standard coverage has effectively collapsed.
*   **Shift in Global Trade Routes:** The persistent danger is forcing a structural shift in energy logistics, with increased reliance on alternative export terminals (like Saudi Arabia’s Yanbu terminal on the Red Sea) and longer voyages around the Cape of Good Hope, which adds millions in costs per transit.
