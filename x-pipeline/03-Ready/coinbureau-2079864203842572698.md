### **Research Brief: Oil Market Volatility (July 22, 2026)**

#### **1. Verification Status**
**TRUE.** As of July 22, 2026, Brent crude futures surged past $95 per barrel, reaching levels not seen since June 11, 2026. Multiple financial news outlets and market data providers confirm that Brent climbed to approximately $95.02–$95.47, while US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) rose to between $87.56 and $88.56 per barrel.

#### **2. Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **Brent Crude:** Traded at ~$95.41/bbl (up ~4.8%) as of mid-day July 22.
*   **WTI Crude:** Traded at ~$88.56/bbl (up ~5%).
*   **Timeline:** This is the highest price point since June 11, 2026.
*   **Conflict Drivers:** 
    *   11th consecutive night of US military strikes on Iranian military and logistics infrastructure.
    *   Houthi rebels in Yemen declared a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia, threatening maritime shipping in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.
    *   Iran-backed forces continue to target tankers in the Strait of Hormuz.

#### **3. Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Recent Lows:** Crude prices were significantly lower in early July 2026; for instance, WTI was trading near $70–$72/bbl around July 2.
*   **Market Expectations:** The EIA had forecasted lower prices (averaging $74/bbl for 3Q26) based on an assumption of re-established trade flows and a June Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) to end the conflict, which has now effectively collapsed.
*   **Volatility:** Prices have risen approximately 20% over the past month alone.

#### **4. Macro/Geopolitical Context**
The conflict has evolved into a "full-scale war" scenario, threatening two critical global energy chokepoints: the **Strait of Hormuz** (handling ~20% of world oil traffic) and the **Bab el-Mandeb Strait**. The collapse of the June ceasefire has triggered renewed risk premiums, with analysts highlighting that supply disruptions could worsen if shipping routes in the Red Sea are further compromised.

#### **5. Most Affected Party & Impact**
*   **Gains:** Energy producers and oil-exporting nations (e.g., non-conflict-affected producers) see immediate revenue windfalls.
*   **Loses/Affected:** 
    *   **Consumers:** Retail gasoline prices are rising (reports indicate >$4/gallon in the US), fueling inflation concerns.
    *   **Importers:** Asian economies heavily reliant on Middle Eastern oil are particularly vulnerable to supply shocks.
    *   **Sectors:** Airlines, transport, chemicals, and consumer-facing sectors face higher input costs, pressuring margins.
    *   **Governments:** Central banks face the dilemma of "sticky" inflation, which may force the postponement of interest rate cuts or even necessitate hikes.

#### **6. Secondary/Hidden Effects**
*   **Stagflation Risks:** The supply shock echoes 1970s energy crisis dynamics, raising risks of stagflation and global recession.
*   **Shipping Rerouting:** Increased insurance premiums and the need to avoid chokepoints are creating significant logistical bottlenecks, affecting not just energy but broader global trade.
*   **Corporate Exposure:** Infrastructure assets (e.g., data centers in the region) are now considered potential targets, increasing operational risk for multinational corporations.
