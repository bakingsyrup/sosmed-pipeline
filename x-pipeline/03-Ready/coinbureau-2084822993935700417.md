# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** Electronic Arts (EA) was officially delisted from the Nasdaq Stock Market effective August 5, 2026, following the completion of its $55 billion acquisition by a consortium led by Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF), alongside Silver Lake and Affinity Partners, on August 4, 2026.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Transaction Value:** $55 billion (all-cash deal).
*   **Shareholder Payout:** $210 per share.
*   **Ownership Structure:** PIF (93.4%), Silver Lake (5.5%), Affinity Partners (1.1%).
*   **Financing:** Approximately $36 billion in equity and $20 billion in debt financing (led by JPMorgan Chase).
*   **Delisting Date:** August 4, 2026 (last trading day); August 5, 2026 (suspension/delisting).
*   **Duration as Public Company:** ~37 years (listed in 1990).

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Premium:** The $210/share price represented a ~25% premium over the unaffected share price of $168.32 (as of September 25, 2025).
*   **Scale:** This transaction is reported as the largest leveraged buyout (LBO) in history, surpassing the 2007 TXU energy buyout. It is the second-largest gaming acquisition in history, behind Microsoft’s acquisition of Activision Blizzard ($68.7 billion).

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** The 2007 TXU Corp buyout (valued at ~$45 billion) was the previous record-holder for the largest leveraged buyout in history.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** The Microsoft-Activision Blizzard deal (2023) established the precedent for massive consolidation in the gaming industry, though it was a strategic corporate acquisition rather than an LBO.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** PIF’s "Savvy Games Group" strategy has involved multi-billion dollar stakes in Nintendo, Capcom, Take-Two, and other gaming entities, signaling a long-term shift by the Saudi sovereign fund to dominate global interactive entertainment.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The acquisition is a cornerstone of Saudi Arabia’s "Vision 2030" initiative, aiming to diversify the kingdom's economy away from oil dependency. By gaining majority control (93.4%) of one of the world's largest video game publishers, PIF positions itself as a dominant force in global digital media, sports, and entertainment.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gainers:** EA shareholders, who received a 25% premium on their shares.
*   **Losers/At Risk:** EA employees, who face potential layoffs and cost-cutting measures due to the massive debt burden (~$20 billion) incurred by the LBO.
*   **Financial Flow:** Cash flowed from the Saudi PIF and its partners to public shareholders, while the company itself now carries significant new debt obligations.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Reduced Transparency:** As a private company, EA is no longer subject to the same rigorous quarterly reporting and transparency requirements as a public company, making it harder for the public and investors to track its financial health.
*   **Cultural Concerns:** Critics have raised concerns regarding "culture washing" and the potential for the Saudi government to influence the content of globally popular franchises (e.g., *EA Sports FC*, *Battlefield*).
*   **Operational Shifts:** The shift to private ownership is intended to allow for long-term R&D investment (specifically in AI and game development) without the pressure of quarterly earnings, but it also creates a "high-stakes wager" where the company must service its debt.
