# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** Alphabet (Google’s parent company) disclosed in its Q2 2026 10-Q filing that it holds a stake in SpaceX valued at approximately **$94.1 billion** as of June 30, 2026. This is the first time the company has officially marked its long-held private investment to public market prices following SpaceX’s initial public offering (IPO) on June 12, 2026.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Current Stake Value:** ~$94.1 billion.
*   **Ownership Percentage:** Approximately 4.9% to 5%.
*   **Investment History:** Google first invested in SpaceX in January 2015, contributing between $500 million and $900 million as part of a $1 billion funding round (partnering with Fidelity) at a valuation of roughly $12 billion.
*   **Lockup Restrictions:** 
    *   **$80 billion** is subject to short-term post-IPO lockup restrictions.
    *   **$14.1 billion** is subject to long-term restrictions through Q3 2027.
*   **Context of Gains:** Alphabet reported nearly $99 billion in total equity securities gains for Q2 2026, driven primarily by its SpaceX stake and its investment in the AI company Anthropic.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Initial Valuation (2015):** SpaceX was valued at approximately $12 billion at the time of Google’s entry.
*   **IPO Valuation (June 2026):** SpaceX’s IPO was the largest in history, pricing at $135/share and valuing the company at approximately $1.77 trillion.
*   **Return on Investment:** The stake has grown roughly 100-fold since the 2015 investment.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
*   **Big Tech & Infrastructure:** The investment highlights the deepening integration between Big Tech and critical infrastructure. Google is not just a financial investor; it has an operational dependency on SpaceX, having signed a multi-year deal to pay SpaceX approximately $920 million per month (totaling ~$30 billion) to lease 110,000 Nvidia GPUs for AI computing capacity.
*   **AI Spending Race:** Alphabet’s massive capital expenditure (capex) guidance—reaching up to $205 billion for 2026—is being heavily scrutinized by investors. The "paper profit" from SpaceX and Anthropic helps offset the negative free cash flow resulting from these massive AI infrastructure investments.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Alphabet (Google):** Gains a massive boost to its balance sheet and net income on paper. However, because the shares are locked up, these are unrealized gains that cannot be used for immediate cash needs.
*   **Alphabet Shareholders:** The volatility of the SpaceX stake (which is now publicly traded) will now directly impact Alphabet’s quarterly earnings reports, introducing a new layer of market risk to Alphabet’s stock price.
*   **SpaceX:** Benefits from a stable, long-term partnership with a tech giant that provides a high-margin, recurring revenue stream via the AI compute leasing agreement.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Accounting Volatility:** Alphabet’s net income is now highly sensitive to SpaceX’s stock performance. Under GAAP rules, unrealized gains/losses must be included in financial reporting, meaning Alphabet’s "profit" could fluctuate wildly based on SpaceX’s share price, even if the underlying business of Google remains stable.
*   **"Paper" vs. "Cash":** Despite the $94 billion windfall, Alphabet reported negative free cash flow and increased its capex, signaling that its actual cash position is under pressure from its AI infrastructure build-out.
*   **Market Overhang:** The significant portion of shares locked under short-term restrictions creates a potential "overhang" risk; once these lockups expire, the market will monitor whether Alphabet intends to liquidate any portion of this stake to fund its own massive capital requirements.
