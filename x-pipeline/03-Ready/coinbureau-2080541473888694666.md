**True.** As of July 24, 2026, the South Korean KOSPI index experienced a sharp decline of 5.72%, closing at 6,690.62. This move follows a period of extreme volatility and a broader "bear market" transition that has seen the index fall approximately 25%–30% from its all-time high in June 2026. The sell-off was indeed led by heavy losses in key semiconductor stocks, specifically Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. Additionally, Brent crude oil prices recently breached the $100/barrel mark due to escalating Middle East tensions, fueling inflationary fears.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **KOSPI Performance:** Dropped 5.72% on July 24, 2026, to 6,690.62. The index is down roughly 25%–30% from its June 2026 peak of ~9,386.
*   **Key Stock Declines (July 24):** Samsung Electronics fell 7.59% and SK Hynix fell 8.29%.
*   **Market Mechanisms:** The Korea Exchange (KRX) triggered "sidecar" trading curbs (halting program selling) on both the KOSPI and KOSDAQ on July 24. This was the 21st time a sell-sidecar was activated on the KOSPI in 2026.
*   **Oil Prices:** Brent crude rose above $100/barrel on July 23–24, 2026, for the first time since May, driven by conflict in the Middle East and tanker attacks in the Red Sea.
*   **Regulatory Action:** The Financial Services Commission (FSC) announced an increase in cash deposit requirements for retail investors trading single-stock leveraged ETFs, from 10 million won to 30 million won, effective July 31.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Year-to-Date:** Despite the recent crash, the KOSPI remains significantly higher than it was a year ago (up over 100% in some tracking metrics), reflecting the massive AI-driven "mad bull" rally that preceded the current rout.
*   **Volatility:** The KOSPI has been more volatile than Bitcoin during certain periods in 2026, with circuit breakers/sidecars being triggered at an unprecedented frequency (38 total trading halts by July 20, 2026).
*   **Previous Highs:** The index hit an all-time high of 9,385.59 on June 19, 2026.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
*   **AI Spending Fatigue:** The market is reacting to concerns that the massive capital expenditure (CapEx) by U.S. tech giants (e.g., Alphabet, Tesla) may not yield sufficient returns, potentially leading to a slowdown in AI chip demand.
*   **Energy Shock:** The return of $100 oil is exacerbating inflationary pressures, complicating central bank efforts to manage interest rates and increasing costs for energy-dependent industries.
*   **Geopolitics:** Escalating conflict between the U.S. and Iran, including attacks on oil tankers in the Red Sea/Strait of Hormuz, has created an energy supply risk premium.
*   **Trade Policy:** New U.S. tariff measures (10%–12.5% on nearly all imports from 60 partners) are adding further uncertainty to South Korea's export-dependent economy.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Retail Investors:** Highly leveraged retail investors are the most affected. Many used margin loans and leveraged ETFs to chase the AI rally; the subsequent crash triggered mass forced liquidations and significant wealth destruction.
*   **Financial Flow:** Foreign investors have turned into net sellers (e.g., selling ~$1.8 billion in a single day on July 24), putting downward pressure on the won and exacerbating the equity sell-off.
*   **Pension/Institutional Funds:** The National Pension Service (NPS) and other local funds have been forced into "mechanical" selling to rebalance portfolios, which has amplified market downdrafts.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Leverage Cascade:** The proliferation of single-stock leveraged ETFs (designed to provide 2x daily returns of Samsung/SK Hynix) has created a structural "feedback loop." These products require end-of-day rebalancing, which forces buying during rallies and selling during crashes, significantly deepening the volatility.
*   **Capital Reallocation:** There are reports of Asian hedge funds selling Korean chip giants to reallocate capital into upcoming IPOs in other regions (e.g., ChangXin Technology).
*   **Regulatory "Chilling" Effect:** The emergency regulatory intervention (raising deposit requirements) is effectively curbing the speculative retail participation that fueled the initial 2026 rally, potentially leading to lower liquidity and a more subdued market environment in the near term.
