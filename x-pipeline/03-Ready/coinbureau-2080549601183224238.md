# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** Poolin Technology and two U.S. affiliates (Lonestar Dream Inc. and Lonestar Taproot LLC) filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey on July 22, 2026. The filing is a liquidation process rather than an attempt to reorganize the business.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Liabilities:** Estimated between $100 million and $500 million, with specific prepetition obligations totaling approximately **$173.1 million**.
*   **Assets:** Estimated at $1 million to $10 million.
*   **Customer Impact:** Roughly **$163.7 million** of the debt consists of unsecured IOUs issued to approximately **11,700 wallet customers** whose withdrawals were frozen in September 2022.
*   **Asset Sale:** Poolin has proposed a "stalking-horse" bid of **$52 million** from Thor CALAP LLC for its two West Texas mining sites (Pyote and Tarbush).
*   **Market Position:** Once the world’s largest Bitcoin mining pool in 2019 (accounting for ~18-20% of global hashrate), it currently holds approximately 0.2% market share (ranked 17th).

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Historical Peak:** In 2019, Poolin was the dominant global mining pool.
*   **Previous Status:** The company effectively ceased normal operations after the 2022 liquidity crisis, during which it halted withdrawals and issued the aforementioned IOUs.
*   **Operational Losses:** The company’s U.S. expansion was hindered by power grid constraints, leading to approximately $46 million in cumulative operational losses across its American subsidiaries and significant losses on hardware sales.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
Poolin’s collapse is the culmination of a multi-year decline triggered by China’s 2021 crypto mining ban, which forced the firm to relocate operations. The subsequent 2022 crypto market crash exposed its lending-heavy business model. The current bankruptcy highlights a broader industry trend where legacy Bitcoin miners are struggling with rising electricity costs and compressed margins, often pivoting their infrastructure toward Artificial Intelligence (AI) and High-Performance Computing (HPC) data centers to remain viable.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Most Affected:** The **11,700 retail wallet holders** who have had their funds frozen since 2022 are the primary losers. 
*   **Financial Flow:** The $52 million stalking-horse bid represents only about 31.8% of the total owed to these creditors before accounting for bankruptcy administrative costs, legal fees, and potential priority claims. Recovery for these users is highly uncertain and will depend on the final auction results and court-ordered payment hierarchies.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **AI Infrastructure Pivot:** The bankruptcy highlights the high demand for existing power-capable sites. The auction process actively courted AI and HPC operators, signaling that physical mining infrastructure (power rights and electrical systems) is increasingly being repurposed for AI, which is often more profitable than Bitcoin mining in the current high-energy-cost environment.
*   **Market Sentiment:** While Poolin’s current hashrate is negligible (0.2%), the bankruptcy of a "founding generation" mining firm serves as a cautionary signal regarding the long-term sustainability of debt-fueled expansion models in the volatile crypto mining sector.
