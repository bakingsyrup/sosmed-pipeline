# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** The U.S. Hispanic Chamber of Commerce (USHCC) sent a letter to Senate leadership around July 21–23, 2026, expressing concerns that the **CLARITY Act** (Digital Asset Market Clarity Act) could accelerate the migration of deposits from traditional, federally insured financial institutions into stablecoin platforms. The USHCC argues this shift could reduce the lending capacity of community banks, which are vital for Hispanic-owned businesses and underserved communities.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **The Core Concern:** Banking groups (including the American Bankers Association and ICBA) and the USHCC fear that stablecoin "rewards" (yield) will attract deposits away from banks.
*   **Legislative Context:** The CLARITY Act aims to create a regulatory framework for digital assets. The current draft reportedly prohibits rewards on "idle" stablecoin balances (deemed functionally equivalent to deposit interest) but permits "activity-based" rewards (tied to transactions or usage).
*   **Scale of Risk:** Banking groups have claimed that as much as **$1.3 trillion in deposits** and **$850 billion in loans** could potentially be at risk due to deposit flight.
*   **Economic Analysis:** A recent White House Council of Economic Advisers report (April 2026) countered these fears, estimating that a full ban on stablecoin yields would only increase bank lending by approximately **$2.1 billion (0.02%)** while costing consumers roughly **$800 million** in lost potential returns.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Previous Legislation:** The **GENIUS Act**, signed in July 2025, established the initial federal framework for payment stablecoins, requiring 1:1 liquid reserves and AML compliance.
*   **Legislative Hurdles:** The CLARITY Act faces a 60-vote threshold in the Senate. Resistance from both sides—banks concerned about deposit flight and some lawmakers concerned about ethics and illicit finance—has jeopardized its passage before the August 2026 recess.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The U.S. is attempting to solidify its leadership in digital finance. While proponents argue that a clear regulatory framework (like the CLARITY Act) is necessary to keep the U.S. competitive globally, traditional banking interests view the "stablecoin-as-deposit" phenomenon as a systemic threat to the traditional fractional-reserve banking model. The debate highlights a structural conflict between the established banking sector and the burgeoning digital asset industry.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Losers:** Community banks and the small businesses (including Hispanic-owned enterprises) that rely on them for credit. If deposits leave, the "loanable funds" available for local small-business lending decrease.
*   **Gains/Neutral:** Large financial institutions (like Goldman Sachs) have shown support for the bill, favoring regulatory clarity over the potential risks of deposit migration. Crypto platforms and stablecoin issuers benefit from a framework that legitimizes their products while allowing for activity-based incentives.
*   **Financial Flow:** Retail and institutional funds flow from traditional bank savings accounts (which may offer lower interest) into stablecoin platforms (which offer higher rewards/yields).

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Credit Availability:** If community banks lose stable deposits, they may tighten lending standards or reduce the volume of loans to minority-owned and low-to-moderate-income businesses, potentially widening the credit access gap.
*   **Regulatory Loophole:** The distinction between "idle balance" rewards and "activity-based" rewards is being heavily litigated in the bill's language. If the definition is too loose, platforms may simply rebrand interest as "activity-based" to bypass restrictions, rendering the banking industry's protections ineffective.
*   **Political Risk:** The opposition from groups like the USHCC complicates the bill's path to the 60-vote threshold, potentially stalling U.S. digital asset regulation indefinitely.
