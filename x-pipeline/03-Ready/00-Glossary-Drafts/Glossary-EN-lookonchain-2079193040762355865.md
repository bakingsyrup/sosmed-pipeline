Bitmine Just Slashed Its Weekly ETH Buy To An All-Time Low

They bought only 7,430 ETH last week, a tiny fraction of the 30K+ batches we saw before.

Today, we will touch the basics of Staking and Liquidity Squeeze to understand how this slowdown actually sets the stage for massive volatility in Ethereum.

When you stake crypto, you lock it inside a proof-of-stake network to help validate transactions and secure the chain. In return, you earn regular rewards, like interest on a savings account. The catch? You can't move those assets quickly; they become illiquid for a while. This turns idle coins into income machines, but it also means that staked tokens disappear from the open market.

Bitmine understands this perfectly. They now hold 5.77 million ETH and have staked 85% of it. That's 4.9 million ETH locked away, earning yield and completely unreachable for panic sales or sudden market orders. This alone removes a massive chunk of the circulating supply from exchanges.

Now imagine a market where a huge player has already tied up most of the available apples in a warehouse. When that same player stops buying the remaining apples on the shelf, demand pressure drops, but the supply is still tiny. This is where liquidity squeeze steps in.

A liquidity squeeze happens when the asset supply on exchanges dries up drastically. Even a small buy order can then send prices flying because there simply aren't enough tokens available at the current price level. Order books become fragile, and volatility becomes the default state.

With Bitmine pivoting capital away from fresh ETH purchases and into its own stock buybacks, the buy-side pressure on ETH disappears. Yet the locked staking supply remains, so the market is left with razor-thin liquidity. If a bullish catalyst hits, prices can spike violently. If panic strikes, the decline can be just as sharp because there’s no institutional cushion ready to absorb the dump.

For us retail traders, this environment is a double-edged sword. Thin liquidity means our positions can get wiped out by sudden wicks, especially if we use leverage. The same mechanism that rewards long-term stakers can destroy short-term speculators. Bitmine’s treasury shift didn’t make ETH safer; it just loaded the volatility cannon while stepping away from the trigger.
