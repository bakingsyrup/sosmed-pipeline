Trump Just Greenlit a Saudi Nuclear Deal

It's a 30-year Section 123 Agreement worth tens of billions.

But the real story is what it does to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.

Let's start with the basics.

A Section 123 Agreement is the legal contract the U.S. signs with any country before transferring nuclear technology.

It sets the rules.

Can they enrich uranium? Can they reprocess spent fuel? Or are they restricted to using the fuel as-is?

The "Gold Standard" version of this agreement explicitly bars enrichment and reprocessing.

The UAE signed that version in 2009.

Saudi Arabia did not.

Their new deal leaves the door open for a domestic enrichment facility.

Now here's why that matters for the NPT.

The Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty is the global rulebook that says: non-nuclear states don't build enrichment capability.

In exchange, they get access to peaceful nuclear technology.

It's a bargain.

The U.S. has spent decades enforcing this bargain against Iran.

Sanctions, pressure, diplomacy—all to stop Iran from enriching.

Now the same U.S. government is helping Saudi Arabia build exactly that capability.

Think about the message this sends to every other NPT signatory.

Why should the UAE keep its "Gold Standard" restrictions?

Why should Turkey or Egypt not demand the same deal?

Why should Iran stop enriching when Saudi Arabia is starting?

This is how a non-proliferation regime unravels.

Not with a bang.

But with one Section 123 Agreement at a time.
