Strategy Just Made a Bold Claim About Bitcoin at $10,000

CEO Phong Le stated the company only worries about debt if Bitcoin falls to the $8,000–$10,000 range, even as their 843,775 BTC stash sits on a $63.69 billion cost basis.

This sounds like confidence. It is actually a quiet shift to a defensive survival loop.

To understand why, we must touch the fundamentals of Net Asset Value and Multiple to Net Asset Value. They form a mechanism that reveals whether Strategy is a fortress or a mirage.

Strategy holds 843,775 Bitcoin worth about $54 billion at current prices. From that, we subtract $6.7 billion in convertible notes and preferred stock obligations. What remains is their Net Asset Value, or NAV. NAV strips away all market narrative and leaves only the raw cash that would remain if the company liquidated everything today and paid every creditor. It is the bedrock number.

But bedrock alone does not justify a high stock price. For that, investors need to believe management can use the bedrock to build a skyscraper. That belief is measured by Multiple to Net Asset Value, or mNAV. mNAV compares the market cap to the NAV. If mNAV sits above 1.0, the market trusts the company to generate additional value beyond its raw holdings. If it slips below 1.0, the market signals that the company is worth less than a pile of its own assets.

Think of a restaurant. Its kitchen equipment, tables, and cash register are worth $500,000. That is its NAV. If someone buys the entire restaurant for $600,000, the mNAV is 1.2. They believe the chef and the menu can produce profits beyond the used fryers. If the offer is only $450,000, the mNAV is 0.9. The market trusts the equipment more than the team running it.

Strategy’s mNAV currently hovers at 1.02. This is the number that makes Phong Le’s $10,000 statement a piece of psychological theater. He is not protecting against liquidation. He is protecting the mNAV. So long as mNAV stays above 1.0, the company can continue issuing new shares and convertible debt to buy more Bitcoin. The moment mNAV slips persistently below 1.0, new investors will ask the obvious question: why should they buy Strategy paper at a premium when they can buy Bitcoin directly for cheaper?

When that happens, the capital pump dies. Strategy can no longer raise fresh equity. It must sell Bitcoin to pay interest and dividends. Those sales push Bitcoin lower, which pushes NAV lower, which sends mNAV deeper into the discount zone. This is the death loop.

And here is the dry finale: Bitcoin does not need to hit $10,000 for Strategy to break. mNAV just needs to hit 0.95 and stay there. The market will do the rest, long before any liquidation price is triggered.
