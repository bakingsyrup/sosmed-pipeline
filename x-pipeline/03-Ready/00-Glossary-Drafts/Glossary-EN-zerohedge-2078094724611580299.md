In this post, we will touch the basics of Information Asymmetry and Risk Premium to understand how political narratives force capital rotation.

A situation where one party in a trade has better, deeper, or faster data than the other party. It dictates who makes money in financial markets. Having access to critical news even a few milliseconds before the general public allows institutional players to front-run trades, capture profits, and avoid risks while everyone else is still processing the information. Imagine playing a game of poker, but one player gets to peek at the next card in the deck before deciding how much to bet. Hedge funds pay millions for direct satellite feeds of crop yields so they can trade wheat futures before the government releases the public agriculture report.

When institutions use this exclusive data to anticipate geopolitical conflicts before the public does, they quickly dump vulnerable assets and demand a higher Risk Premium.

The extra return investors demand for holding a risky asset instead of a safe one. It prices fear into the market. When geopolitical tensions rise, investors require higher compensation to hold volatile assets like equities, pushing prices down while driving capital into safe havens like government bonds. Imagine lending money to a friend who always pays you back versus a stranger. You would charge the stranger a higher interest rate because there is a higher chance they might run away with your money. When US-China trade tensions spike, investors demand a higher yield to hold Asian equities, causing the Nikkei and Hang Seng indexes to drop as capital flees to US Treasuries.

The loop is simple: institutional players use early intelligence to adjust their portfolios, pricing in new geopolitical fears, which forces everyday investors to suffer sudden portfolio drops as the market demands higher compensation for risk.
