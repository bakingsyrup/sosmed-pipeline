### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** During World War II, the U.S. implemented yield curve control (YCC) to finance military spending by capping borrowing costs. Japan’s own YCC framework (2016–2024) serves as a modern precedent for aggressive central bank intervention to suppress yields.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** The current Japanese strategy mirrors past periods of "fiscal dominance," where the central bank is forced to maintain accommodative policy to ensure debt sustainability despite inflationary pressures.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** The BOJ has frequently intervened in foreign exchange markets to stem yen weakness (which recently hit a 40-year low of ~163 per dollar). Markets are now testing whether the BOJ will prioritize "yield management" over "FX management."

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The rise in yields reflects a "credibility test" for central banks globally. Rising energy prices (Brent >$100) are fueling inflation expectations. In Japan, this is compounded by the "Basic Policy shock"—a government blueprint that markets feared would prioritize aggressive spending over fiscal discipline, complicating the BOJ’s efforts to hike rates without causing a debt-servicing crisis.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Losers:** Bondholders (due to price drops), the Japanese government (higher debt-servicing costs), and companies reliant on cheap credit.
*   **Gains/Financial Flow:** Investors demanding higher risk premiums for long-term Japanese debt; potential capital rotation out of Japanese stocks (Nikkei) as domestic borrowing costs rise.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
The rise in yields may force the Government Pension Investment Fund (GPIF)—the world's largest—to reallocate capital. If the government moves from "FX management" (capping the dollar-yen rate) to "yield management" (capping 10-year yields), the yen could face further depreciation despite official interventions, potentially leading to a feedback loop of imported inflation.
