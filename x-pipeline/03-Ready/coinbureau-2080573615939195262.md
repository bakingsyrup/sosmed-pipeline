# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**Status: True.**
The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) officially announced on July 23, 2026, that it will host a public roundtable on **September 17, 2026**, to discuss the transition of U.S. equity markets toward 24-hour trading. The event will take place at the SEC’s headquarters in Washington, D.C., and will be live-streamed. The discussion will focus on infrastructure requirements, operational resilience, and investor protections necessary to support a continuous, round-the-clock market.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Event Date:** September 17, 2026 (10:00 AM – 4:00 PM ET).
*   **Objective:** To examine the implications of moving U.S. stock markets to a 24-hour model, aligning them with global markets and crypto-style trading.
*   **Industry Movement:** 
    *   **NYSE Arca:** Planning 23-hour weekday trading.
    *   **Nasdaq:** Engaging with regulators to enable 24-hour trading, aiming for a rollout in the second half of 2026.
    *   **Cboe:** Planning a near 24/5 trading model, pending regulatory approval.
    *   **London Stock Exchange (LSE):** Developing "LSE 24" for 24-hour trading of exchange-traded products, with testing expected by late 2026 and launch in the first half of 2027.
    *   **Blue Ocean ATS:** Has been operating an overnight U.S. stock trading venue since 2021.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Traditional Hours:** For over 40 years, U.S. exchanges have operated on a fixed schedule (9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET, Monday–Friday).
*   **Market Context:** The push for 24/7 trading is largely a response to the "always-on" nature of cryptocurrency markets and the rising demand from retail investors for instant access, which traditional exchanges have historically ignored or limited to pre-market/after-hours sessions.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The shift toward 24-hour trading represents a modernization of U.S. capital markets to maintain competitiveness in a globalized, digital economy. By aligning with the 24/7 nature of digital assets and international trading venues, U.S. exchanges hope to capture volume that currently flows into fragmented, less regulated, or offshore venues. It also reflects the broader "tokenization" trend where real-world assets (RWA) are increasingly expected to mirror the liquidity and accessibility of crypto-native assets.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Retail Investors:** These are the primary beneficiaries, gaining the ability to react to news and market events regardless of time zone or traditional market hours.
*   **Market Makers & Institutional Firms:** They face the highest operational impact. They must build or upgrade infrastructure to handle continuous risk management, clearing, and settlement without the "safety net" of market closures.
*   **Financial Flow:** The flow shifts from centralized, time-gated trading sessions toward a continuous stream of capital. This increases the potential for higher trading volumes but also introduces "gap risk"—where prices move significantly during overnight hours when liquidity may be thinner.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Increased Volatility/Liquidity Fragility:** While 24-hour trading offers access, overnight liquidity is often lower than during core hours. This can lead to flash volatility if significant news breaks during low-volume periods.
*   **Clearing & Settlement Complexity:** The current U.S. financial plumbing (clearinghouses, T+1 settlement cycles) is built for traditional business days. Moving to 24/7 requires a total overhaul of back-office processes, which could expose the market to new systemic risks if not handled with extreme technological rigor.
*   **Corporate Governance:** Companies may face pressure to release material news outside of traditional market hours to avoid "opening bell" shocks, fundamentally changing how earnings and corporate events are communicated.
