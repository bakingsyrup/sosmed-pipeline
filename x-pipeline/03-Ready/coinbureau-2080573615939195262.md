# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has officially announced it will host a public roundtable on **September 17, 2026**, to discuss the transition of U.S. equity markets toward 24-hour trading. The discussion will focus on the operational, structural, and regulatory requirements for such a shift, including clearing, settlement, and investor protection.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Event Date:** September 17, 2026.
*   **File Number:** 4-913.
*   **Market Context:** Several major venues are already moving toward extended hours:
    *   **Nasdaq:** Engaging with regulators for 23-hour trading (aiming for H2 2026).
    *   **NYSE Arca:** Approved for 22-hour trading.
    *   **Cboe:** Has filed/is seeking approval for near 24/5 trading.
    *   **24X National Exchange:** Has received SEC approval for extended hours.
    *   **London Stock Exchange (LSE):** Plans to launch a "night-time" trading venue (LSE 24) for exchange-traded products by early 2027.
    *   **Blue Ocean ATS:** Has operated overnight U.S. stock trading since 2021.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Current Standard:** Traditional U.S. equity markets primarily operate from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET.
*   **Benchmark:** Cryptocurrency markets operate on a 24/7 basis, providing a structural benchmark for "always-on" liquidity and clearing that traditional markets are now attempting to emulate.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The shift toward 24-hour trading represents a broader modernization of financial market infrastructure to remain competitive with digital asset markets. As global capital flows become increasingly continuous, U.S. regulators are under pressure to ensure that domestic markets do not lose relevance or liquidity to offshore or crypto-native venues that never close.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Retail Investors:** Gain the ability to react to news events in real-time regardless of time zone, potentially reducing the "gap risk" associated with overnight price movements.
*   **Market Infrastructure (Clearinghouses/Broker-Dealers):** Most affected by the technical and regulatory burden. Firms must upgrade clearing/settlement systems (e.g., DTCC/NSCC), risk management, and surveillance to handle continuous operations without liquidity bottlenecks.
*   **Financial Flow:** Capital currently routed through crypto or international venues during U.S. "off-hours" may be recaptured by U.S. exchanges, centralizing liquidity and increasing trading volumes for institutional and retail participants.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Liquidity Fragmentation:** As trading hours expand, liquidity may spread thin across different time sessions, potentially leading to increased volatility during "low-volume" overnight hours.
*   **Structural Risks:** The reliance on automated systems for 24-hour trading increases the risk of "flash crashes" during times when human oversight is minimal, necessitating more robust algorithmic guardrails.
