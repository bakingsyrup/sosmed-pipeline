# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** On July 23, 2026, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) officially announced that it will host a public roundtable on September 17, 2026, to discuss the transition toward 24-hour trading in U.S. equity markets. The roundtable, identified by **File Number 4-913**, will be held at the SEC’s Washington, D.C. headquarters and livestreamed.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Event Date:** September 17, 2026.
*   **Core Objective:** Discuss infrastructure, operational resiliency, clearing/settlement requirements, and investor protection in an "always-on" market.
*   **Market Context:** Major exchanges are actively seeking to extend hours. Nasdaq has engaged regulators regarding a 23-hour trading model (aiming for late 2026), and NYSE Arca has proposed 22-hour trading. The London Stock Exchange (LSE) is also planning a 24/5 "LSE 24" venue for ETPs.
*   **Current Status:** The U.S. national market system currently operates primarily from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET, supplemented by fragmented pre-market and after-hours sessions.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Historical Norm:** Since 1985, U.S. equity markets have maintained a standard 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET session.
*   **Competitor Benchmark:** Cryptocurrency markets have operated on a 24/7 basis since inception, creating a competitive pressure point for traditional exchanges to modernize to retain retail and institutional liquidity.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The shift toward 24-hour trading represents a broader modernization of financial market infrastructure. As global capital flows become increasingly electronic and continuous, the "gaps" in traditional market hours create inefficiencies, such as delayed price discovery and liquidity bottlenecks for international investors. This move aligns U.S. equities with the "always-on" nature of digital assets and modern algorithmic/agentic trading environments.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gains:** Retail investors (increased flexibility), international investors (ability to trade without relying on illiquid off-hours), and exchanges (increased transaction volume and fee potential).
*   **Affected/Strained:** Broker-dealers and clearinghouses. These institutions must overhaul their "end-of-day" processes, risk management, and staffing to ensure systems remain resilient during overnight hours.
*   **Financial Flow:** Capital that previously sat idle or moved to offshore/crypto venues during U.S. market "off-hours" will likely remain within the regulated U.S. ecosystem, potentially increasing total market liquidity and volume.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Operational Risk:** Continuous trading reduces the "cooling-off" period between sessions, potentially increasing the speed at which volatility can cascade through global markets.
*   **Regulatory Arbitrage:** As exchanges move to 24/5 or 24/7, the need for harmonized global clearing and settlement rules increases, potentially putting pressure on regulators to accelerate the transition to T+0 (instant) settlement to mitigate overnight counterparty risk.
