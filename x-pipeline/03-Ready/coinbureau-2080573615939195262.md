# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**Status: True.** The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has officially announced that it will host a public roundtable on **September 17, 2026**, at its Washington, D.C. headquarters to discuss the transition of U.S. equity markets toward 24-hour trading.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Event:** SEC Roundtable on Preparations for 24-Hour Trading (File Number 4-913).
*   **Context:** Major exchanges are actively pursuing extended hours (typically 23/5 models):
    *   **NYSE Arca:** Planning 23-hour weekday trading (targeting 2026 launch).
    *   **Nasdaq:** Engaging with regulators to provide 23-hour trading, 5 days a week (targeting December 6, 2026 launch).
    *   **Cboe:** Planning near-24/5 trading, pending regulatory approval (targeting December 2026).
    *   **London Stock Exchange (LSEG):** Announced "LSE 24," a 24/5 venue for exchange-traded products (ETPs), with testing in late 2026 and launch in H1 2027.
    *   **Blue Ocean ATS:** Has already been providing overnight U.S. stock trading since 2021.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Traditional Market Hours:** For over 40 years, U.S. exchanges have operated on a fixed schedule of 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET, Monday–Friday.
*   **Market Drivers:** The shift is largely driven by retail investor demand for "crypto-style" access, allowing users to react to breaking news immediately rather than waiting for market opens.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The move represents a modernization of financial infrastructure to align with global, 24/7 digital asset markets. It marks a shift away from concentrated price discovery toward continuous, fragmented liquidity. This is part of a broader trend toward "always-on" finance, where traditional exchanges compete with crypto and decentralized venues for 24/7 retail engagement.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Retail Investors:** Gain: Immediate access to trade during global news cycles. Lose: Higher risk of executing trades in low-liquidity environments, leading to wider bid-offer spreads and increased volatility.
*   **Broker-Dealers:** Impact: Must overhaul internal systems, risk controls, and overnight staffing to maintain operational resilience.
*   **Exchanges:** Impact: Need to balance innovation (attracting international volume) with the requirement to maintain the integrity and stability of the national market system.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Liquidity Fragmentation:** As trading spreads across more hours, volume during the "core" session may thin out, potentially making it harder to fill large institutional orders without moving prices.
*   **Algorithmic/Agentic Dominance:** 24/7 markets favor automated trading systems that can operate without human oversight, potentially widening the gap between institutional/algorithmic traders and manual retail participants.
*   **Reduced "Gap" Risk:** Traditional "overnight risk" (price jumps between market close and open) will be replaced by continuous price discovery, forcing active risk management.
