# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) officially announced on July 23, 2026, that it will host a public roundtable on September 17, 2026, to discuss the transition toward 24-hour trading in U.S. equity markets. The event is formally listed under File Number 4-913.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Roundtable Date:** September 17, 2026.
*   **Core Discussion Topics:** Operational resilience, clearing and settlement requirements, broker-dealer responsibilities, and investor protection in an "always-on" market.
*   **Industry Movement:** Several major venues are actively pursuing extended hours. Nasdaq has proposed a 23-hour, 5-day-a-week model, while NYSE Arca has moved to extend its hours toward a 22-hour model. The London Stock Exchange (LSE) recently announced "LSE 24," a venue for near-continuous trading expected to begin trials in late 2026 for an early 2027 launch.
*   **Precedent:** Blue Ocean ATS has been providing overnight U.S. stock trading since 2021.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Traditional Hours:** Since 1985, standard U.S. equity market hours have been fixed at 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET, Monday through Friday. 
*   **Competitive Pressure:** The move is largely driven by the desire of traditional exchanges to compete with the 24/7 nature of cryptocurrency markets and global demand for continuous response to news.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The shift represents a fundamental modernization of financial market infrastructure. As global capital flows become increasingly electronic and decentralized, the "closing bell" model is viewed as a legacy constraint. This aligns with a broader push by regulators to integrate digital asset-style efficiency into traditional finance (TradFi), potentially compressing the "liquidity premium" currently enjoyed by crypto-native platforms.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Retail Investors:** Gain the ability to react to news and volatility outside of standard hours, but face risks of lower liquidity, wider bid-ask spreads, and increased volatility.
*   **Broker-Dealers & Clearing Houses:** Face the highest operational burden, as they must adapt settlement cycles, risk management, and surveillance systems to function continuously.
*   **Market Makers:** Will likely see increased costs in maintaining liquidity during low-volume "overnight" hours, which may be passed on to retail users as wider spreads.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Liquidity Fragmentation:** As trading spreads across more hours, volume may thin out, potentially making price discovery less efficient during traditional "off-hours".
*   **Erosion of Crypto Advantage:** One of the core value propositions of crypto-native assets—24/7 liquidity—is being neutralized by TradFi, which could impact the narrative premium of RWA (real-world asset) tokenization projects that previously relied on "24/7 access" as a primary differentiator.
