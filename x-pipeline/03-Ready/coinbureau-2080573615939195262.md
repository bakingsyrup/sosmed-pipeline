# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
The claim that major stock exchanges are moving toward 24-hour (or extended-hour) trading and that the SEC has scheduled a roundtable to discuss this shift is **True**.

### 1. Verification Status
*   **Status**: Confirmed. On July 23, 2026, the SEC officially announced a public roundtable to be held on **September 17, 2026**, at its Washington, D.C. headquarters to discuss the transition of U.S. equity markets toward 24-hour trading.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Event Date**: September 17, 2026.
*   **SEC Chair**: Paul S. Atkins.
*   **Major Exchange Initiatives**:
    *   **NYSE Arca**: Planning 23-hour weekday trading.
    *   **Nasdaq**: Targeting December 6, 2026, for 23-hour, five-day-a-week trading.
    *   **Cboe**: Targeting December 2026 for near-24/5 trading (pending approval).
    *   **London Stock Exchange (LSE)**: Announced "LSE 24" for near-round-the-clock weekday trading; testing starts late 2026, with ETPs expected in H1 2027.
*   **Existing Infrastructure**: Blue Ocean ATS has provided overnight U.S. stock trading since 2021.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   Historically, U.S. markets have operated on limited windows (typically 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET). 
*   The push for 24/7 access is a direct competitive response to the "crypto-style" 24/7 market accessibility and the growth of platforms like Robinhood (which has offered 24-hour access to select stocks for three years) and decentralized perpetual futures platforms.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor)**: The transition mirrors the shift from floor-based "open outcry" trading to electronic matching engines in the 1990s/2000s, which similarly faced concerns over liquidity and market stability.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook)**: The "crypto-native" model of 24/7/365 trading forced traditional finance (TradFi) to innovate. This is a classic "disruption-response" cycle where centralized exchanges adopt the operational features of their decentralized competitors to retain retail volume.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor)**: Under SEC Chair Paul S. Atkins, the Commission has signaled a proactive regulatory agenda for 2026, including the modernization of market infrastructure and explicit support for tokenized securities and DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology).

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The shift reflects a move toward "Global Market Integration," where U.S. equities must compete for capital flows against digital assets and international venues that never close. It also addresses the demand from global investors to react to geopolitical shocks or earnings news in real-time, regardless of time zone.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Winners**: Retail investors (greater access), exchanges (increased trading fee revenue from extended hours), and brokerages (deeper customer engagement).
*   **Losers/Risks**: Market makers and liquidity providers (may face wider bid-ask spreads and higher volatility during low-volume overnight hours), and potentially RWA (Real-World Asset) tokenization platforms, which previously used "24/7 trading" as a key differentiator.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Liquidity Fragmentation**: Trading volume may become thinner during off-peak hours, leading to "gaps" in pricing and increased susceptibility to flash volatility driven by news headlines.
*   **Operational Strain**: Clearing and settlement systems (like DTCC) are still largely T+1 or T+0, meaning that while *trading* may be 24/7, the *settlement* infrastructure remains bound by traditional banking hours/constraints.
