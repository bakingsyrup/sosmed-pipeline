# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
The claim regarding the Zcash "Ironwood" upgrade is **true**.

### 1. Verification Status
The Ironwood upgrade (NU6.3) is scheduled for activation on **July 28, 2026, at block height 3,428,143** (approximately 12:00–13:00 UTC). The upgrade is a direct response to a critical "infinity bug" discovered in the Orchard shielded pool on May 29, 2026, by researcher Taylor Hornby. While the vulnerability was patched via an emergency fix in early June 2026, the privacy nature of the Orchard pool meant it was impossible to cryptographically verify if the bug had been exploited before the patch. Ironwood effectively seals the old Orchard pool and forces a migration through an "accounting turnstile," which prevents more ZEC from exiting the pool than legally entered, thereby ensuring the supply integrity of the network.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Upgrade Name**: Ironwood (NU6.3).
*   **Activation Date**: July 28, 2026.
*   **Activation Block**: 3,428,143.
*   **Bug Discovery Date**: May 29, 2026.
*   **Price Impact**: ZEC crashed ~50% (from ~$602 to ~$299) following the June 2026 disclosure, subsequently recovering to trade near $490–$580 in July 2026.
*   **Mechanism**: A "turnstile" migration that enforces strict supply bounds, preventing potential counterfeit ZEC from entering the new pool.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Historical Price**: ZEC was trading near $602 prior to the June 2026 bug disclosure.
*   **Post-Crash Low**: $299.
*   **Recovery**: ZEC rebounded by ~80% from its June 5 intraday low of ~$252–$300.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor)**: The closest parallel is the "Monero" or "Zcash" emergency patches for critical cryptographic flaws, but the "infinite minting" risk in a privacy pool is unprecedented in its scale for Zcash. The most significant historical "inflationary bug" was the Bitcoin "Value Overflow Incident" (2010), where 184 billion BTC were created and subsequently rolled back by a hard fork.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook)**: The "turnstile" migration is a standard industry response to supply-integrity issues in privacy coins, similar to how projects isolate "tainted" or "unverifiable" state segments during major protocol transitions.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor)**: Zcash developers (Electric Coin Co./Zcash Foundation) have a track record of using "emergency network upgrades" to address critical vulnerabilities, having successfully executed the June 2026 two-phase fix (disabling the pool, then patching).

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The incident highlights the inherent tension between "privacy-at-all-costs" and "monetary auditability." In a geopolitical environment where privacy coins face increasing regulatory scrutiny (e.g., Russia’s restrictions on privacy coins), Zcash's ability to prove supply integrity is essential for institutional survival and long-term viability.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gainers**: Long-term holders who benefit from the restoration of "provable" supply, increasing the asset's credibility as a store of value.
*   **Losers**: Short-term traders caught in the volatility of the June crash and potential liquidity fragmentation during the migration.
*   **Financial Flow**: The "turnstile" acts as a filter; if fake ZEC exists, it will be trapped in the legacy Orchard pool, effectively "burning" the counterfeit value and protecting the circulating supply of the new pool.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
The migration may force a "network reset," potentially causing temporary service interruptions for exchanges and wallets that have not upgraded to the new Z3 infrastructure (Zebra nodes).
