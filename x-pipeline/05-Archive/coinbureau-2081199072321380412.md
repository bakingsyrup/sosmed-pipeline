# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**Status: True.** As of July 26, 2026, Yemen’s Houthi rebels have declared a maritime blockade on Saudi Arabia, specifically targeting the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. Multiple reports confirm that oil tankers have reversed course or altered plans following the announcement on July 20, 2026. Global energy markets are currently reacting to the heightened risk of a dual-chokepoint crisis (Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb), with Brent crude prices recently breaching $100/barrel.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **The Blockade:** Houthis announced an "effective immediately" maritime embargo on Saudi-linked shipping through the Bab al-Mandeb on July 20, 2026.
*   **Tanker Reversals:** At least 4–5 tankers carrying Saudi-origin cargo (crude, gasoil, naphtha) reversed course or halted near the Red Sea/Gulf of Aden following the threat.
*   **Export Volume:** Saudi Arabia’s exports from the Red Sea port of Yanbu had surged to ~3.8–4 million barrels per day (bpd) as an alternative to the Hormuz-constrained Gulf routes.
*   **Global Impact:** The Bab al-Mandeb facilitates ~12% of global oil shipments. Analysts (e.g., Rystad Energy) warn that a combined blockade of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb could impact ~25% of global oil supply.
*   **Market Price:** Brent crude surged over 13% in recent days, surpassing $100/barrel.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Peacetime Transit:** Prior to the 2023/2024 conflict, 65–72 commercial vessels typically transited the Bab al-Mandeb daily.
*   **Pre-Crisis Yanbu Exports:** Before the current geopolitical escalation, Yanbu exports were ~240,000 bpd; they rose to ~3.5–4 million bpd in mid-2026 as a critical relief valve.
*   **Voyage Time:** Rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope adds ~30 days to the standard ~20-day transit to Asia, roughly doubling the voyage time and significantly increasing freight/insurance costs.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** The 2023–2024 Red Sea crisis provides the most direct precedent. During that period, Houthi attacks led to a massive rerouting of global shipping away from the Suez Canal, causing a sustained increase in global freight rates and insurance premiums.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** The "Dark Fleet" strategy—where vessels turn off transponders to bypass sanctions or avoid targeting—has been observed again in the current crisis, with some Saudi-linked vessels going "dark" to attempt passage.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** The Houthis successfully demonstrated the ability to disrupt global trade in 2023–2024 via drone and missile strikes. Their current "ban" on Saudi-linked ports escalates this by specifically targeting the kingdom's primary "Plan B" export infrastructure.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The situation represents a "pincer movement" on global energy supply. With the Strait of Hormuz already experiencing severe disruption due to Iran-U.S. hostilities, Saudi Arabia’s reliance on the Yanbu pipeline (Red Sea) became its primary survival mechanism. By targeting the Bab al-Mandeb, the Houthis aim to close the only remaining major exit for Saudi crude, potentially forcing a global energy supply shock.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Losers:** Saudi Arabia (export capacity/revenue), global consumers (higher fuel/energy prices), and shipping companies (soaring insurance/security costs).
*   **Financial Flow:** Capital is flowing away from "high-risk" shipping routes toward higher insurance premiums, increased bunker fuel consumption (due to longer routes), and higher commodity spot prices.
*   **Gains:** Iran (strategic leverage), and potentially oil-producing nations not reliant on these specific chokepoints.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Artificial Scarcity:** A significant portion of the price spike is driven by "risk premium" rather than physical supply loss, as tankers are idling rather than being destroyed.
*   **Logistics Bottleneck:** The sudden influx of vessels attempting to route around Africa (Cape of Good Hope) is creating secondary congestion in other global ports and increasing global demand for bunkering fuel.
