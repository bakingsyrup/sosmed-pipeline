# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** On July 24, 2026, the Seoul High Court ordered SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won to pay his former wife, Roh Soh-yeong, **944 billion Korean won (approximately $644 million)** in a property division settlement. This ruling follows a remand from the Supreme Court and is widely recognized as the largest divorce settlement in South Korean history.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Settlement Amount**: 944 billion KRW (~$644 million).
*   **Payment Method**: Cash (not stock), to preserve Chey’s management control over SK Group.
*   **Asset Division Ratio**: The court set the division at two-thirds for Chey and one-third for Roh.
*   **Context of Increase**: The award is ~14 times higher than the initial 2022 trial court ruling (66.5 billion KRW) and represents a reduction from a previous 2024 appellate ruling (1.38 trillion KRW) that was partially overturned by the Supreme Court.
*   **Net Worth Impact**: The payout represents roughly 12% of Chey’s estimated $5.6 billion fortune.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Previous Award (2024)**: 1.38 trillion KRW (~$940–$945 million).
*   **Original Trial Court Award (2022)**: 66.5 billion KRW (~$48 million at the time).
*   **Market Expectation**: Analysts and observers were closely watching whether the ruling would force Chey to liquidate shares, which could have triggered a major shift in the ownership structure of South Korea’s second-largest conglomerate. The court's decision to mandate a cash payment was specifically designed to mitigate this risk.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor)**: This is the largest divorce settlement in South Korean history, surpassing the previous record (the 2024 appellate ruling of 1.38 trillion KRW, which was later set aside).
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook)**: In the early 2000s (2003), Chey Tae-won faced a hostile takeover attempt by Sovereign Asset Management, which sought to oust him by acquiring a 15% stake. Chey successfully defended his management rights, and the current divorce ruling specifically accounts for this "management control" priority by ordering a cash payout instead of a share transfer.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor)**: Chey Tae-won’s personal wealth has seen extreme volatility linked to the performance of SK Hynix. His net worth grew from ~$1 billion at the start of 2025 to over $5 billion in 2026, driven largely by the AI-chip demand boom.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The case highlights the intersection of "chaebol" (family-run conglomerate) governance and personal liability. SK Group is a central player in the global AI supply chain, particularly through SK Hynix, a leading supplier of High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) for Nvidia. The court's ruling acknowledges the role of the "AI boom" in inflating the value of marital assets, effectively linking personal divorce settlements to global semiconductor market cycles.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gains**: Roh Soh-yeong receives the largest divorce settlement in Korean history.
*   **Loses/Affected**: Chey Tae-won must raise $644 million in cash. This creates a liquidity burden that may require him to borrow against his assets, sell non-core holdings, or pledge more shares, though it prevents the immediate loss of voting control over SK Group.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Corporate Governance**: The ruling sets a legal precedent that a spouse’s contributions (including homemaking and external support) can entitle them to a significant share of "inherited" or "management-linked" assets if those assets grew during the marriage.
*   **Liquidity Risk**: The requirement to pay in cash could lead to "crowding out" of other capital investments if Chey is forced to divert dividends or leverage his personal stake to secure the required liquidity.
