# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### Verification Status: **TRUE**
Alphabet Inc. (Google's parent company) officially disclosed in its Q2 2026 10-Q filing (released July 23, 2026) that it holds approximately **$94.1 billion** in SpaceX (SPCX) shares.

### Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Holding Value:** $94.1 billion.
*   **Ownership Stake:** Approximately 4.9% to 5.0% of SpaceX.
*   **Lock-up Status:**
    *   **$80 billion** is subject to short-term sale restrictions (standard post-IPO lock-up).
    *   **$14.1 billion** is subject to long-term restrictions through Q3 2027.
*   **Investment History:** Google was an early investor, participating in a ~$1 billion funding round in 2015 alongside Fidelity, with Google contributing roughly $500–$900 million at a valuation of approximately $12 billion.
*   **Market Context:** SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history on June 12, 2026, at a valuation of $1.77 trillion. As of late July 2026, the stock has faced downward pressure, trading below its $135 IPO price.

### Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Investment Growth:** The original 2015 investment has seen an approximate 100-fold return in paper value.
*   **Alphabet Financials:** Alphabet reported nearly $100 billion in unrealized gains from equity investments for Q2 2026, significantly boosting its net income.
*   **Operational Spend:** Alphabet has guided toward $205 billion in total capital expenditure for 2026, primarily focused on AI data centers.

### Macro/Geopolitical Context
SpaceX has pivoted from being primarily a launch provider to an integrated "Space, Connectivity, and AI" platform. The investment ties Google’s AI ambitions directly to SpaceX’s infrastructure. Google has reportedly entered a massive commercial agreement to lease compute capacity (Nvidia GPUs) from SpaceX for nearly $1 billion per month through June 2029.

### Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Alphabet (Google):** Gains a massive, albeit currently illiquid, paper asset that buffers against high capital expenditures. However, they are also a major customer, paying SpaceX billions for compute access, creating a complex "investor-tenant" dependency.
*   **Retail/Institutional Investors:** Those who bought into the SpaceX IPO are currently facing volatility as the stock trades below its $135 debut price and faces a "lock-up cliff" in August 2026, where over $116 billion in stock may become eligible for sale.

### Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Balance Sheet Sensitivity:** Google’s balance sheet is now sensitive to SpaceX’s operational performance (e.g., Starship launch success/failure) and market valuation, as the stake is too large to be a "rounding error."
*   **Liquidity Risk:** Because the majority of the stake is locked up, Google cannot easily liquidate this position to fund its own massive AI infrastructure build-out, meaning it must continue to rely on its core advertising revenue and debt to fund its $205 billion capex guidance.
*   **Strategic Leverage:** The $1 billion/month compute-leasing deal gives SpaceX significant leverage over Google, as Google is effectively renting the very AI infrastructure it needs to remain competitive in the search and generative AI arms race.
