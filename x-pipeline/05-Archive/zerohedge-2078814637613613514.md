# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### **1. Verification Status**
**True.** The French National Gambling Authority (*Autorité nationale des jeux* or **ANJ**) officially ordered internet service providers (ISPs) to block access to the Polymarket website on July 16, 2026. This action escalates a previous, less restrictive measure implemented in November 2024.

---

### **2. Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **The Trigger:** Despite a prior ban on financial transactions (November 2024), traffic from French IP addresses continued to rise. The ANJ recorded **578,751 visits** from **205,057 unique visitors** in June 2026 alone.
*   **Legal Basis:** The ANJ classified the platform as an unauthorized gambling service. Under French law, promoting such a service—including simply displaying live odds—is a criminal offense punishable by fines of up to **€100,000 (~$114,000)**.
*   **Specific Incidents:**
    *   **Meteo-France:** A complaint was filed in April 2026 after a weather probe was allegedly hacked to manipulate outcomes for weather-related betting markets.
    *   **Market Manipulation:** Regulators pointed to cases where large-scale betting (e.g., the "Fredi9999" account) significantly shifted market odds.

---

### **3. Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Prior Status:** Since November 2024, French users were technically restricted to "view-only" mode, and payments were blocked. The recent decision represents a shift from "transactional restriction" to a "total access block" via ISPs.
*   **Global Context:** Polymarket is already geo-blocked in over 35 jurisdictions, including Brazil, Italy, Poland, and Spain (which temporarily banned both Polymarket and Kalshi in May 2026).

---

### **4. Macro/Geopolitical Context**
This move is part of a broader European and Western regulatory crackdown on "prediction markets." Regulators are increasingly struggling to categorize these platforms:
*   **Gambling vs. Finance:** While these platforms market themselves as "information markets," gambling regulators (like the ANJ) are winning the jurisdictional battle by classifying them as illegal betting, while financial regulators are simultaneously evaluating whether these contracts constitute unregulated derivatives or securities.
*   **National Security:** The involvement of cybercrime authorities (e.g., the Office for Combating Cybercrime) indicates that governments view these platforms not just as financial risks, but as vectors for market manipulation, information warfare, and potential threats to public interest/national data integrity.

---

### **5. Most Affected Party & Impact**
*   **Primary Loser (Platform):** Polymarket loses access to a significant European market, threatening its growth trajectory and potentially triggering similar "domino effect" bans in other EU member states that look to the ANJ’s regulatory framework for guidance.
*   **Primary Loser (Users):** French retail users who relied on the platform for hedging or speculative betting are now forced to use VPNs or move to less regulated alternatives, exposing them to higher risks.
*   **Financial Flow:** The direct flow of capital from French retail participants into the Polymarket ecosystem is being severed. The government gains tighter control over domestic gambling revenue, steering it toward licensed, tax-paying domestic sportsbooks.

---

### **6. Secondary/Hidden Effects**
*   **The "Whack-a-Mole" Reality:** As seen with previous blocks, determined users often circumvent ISP-level blocks via DNS changes or VPNs. The effectiveness of this ban will likely remain partial unless the regulator pursues aggressive enforcement against the platform's payment rails or marketing affiliates.
*   **Regulatory Convergence:** The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) has signaled that prediction markets may be treated similarly to binary options, which are already largely banned for retail investors in the EU. This suggests a long-term, structural threat to the business model of prediction markets across the entire European market.
