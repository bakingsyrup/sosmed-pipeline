# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### **1. Verification Status**
**True.** On July 15, 2026, the South Korean Ministry of Economy and Finance announced plans to introduce the **National Asset Basic Act**, which will modernize the country’s state asset management framework to include cryptocurrencies (virtual assets) and other modern asset classes like intellectual property. This replaces the outdated *State Property Act* (enacted in 1950), which was limited to real estate.

### **2. Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **Policy Change:** The government is shifting its framework from a "preservation/sale" focus to "value creation," officially recognizing digital assets within the national fiscal system.
*   **Legislative Timeline:** The government aims to advance the **Digital Asset Basic Act** and the **National Asset Basic Act** in the second half of 2026.
*   **Related Initiatives:**
    *   **Spot Bitcoin ETFs:** The government is actively working to revise the *Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act* to allow for domestic spot virtual asset ETFs.
    *   **Stablecoin Framework:** Plans include specific licensing, reserve requirements, and custody standards for stablecoin issuers.
    *   **Capital Requirements:** A minimum capital requirement of **KRW 500 million (approx. $360,000)** is being set for certain digital asset issuers.
*   **Market Context:** South Korea represents approximately 15% to 20% of global crypto trading volume.

### **3. Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Legacy Framework:** The previous *State Property Act* (1950) was designed for a post-war economy where government assets were almost exclusively real estate.
*   **Previous Legislative Steps:** The *Act on the Protection of Virtual Asset Users* (July 2024) focused primarily on investor protection, anti-money laundering (AML), and market integrity. The 2026 developments represent a shift toward **institutionalization** and **fiscal integration**.

### **4. Macro/Geopolitical Context**
South Korea is aggressively positioning itself as a leader in the digital financial economy. By integrating crypto into state asset management, the government is signaling that digital assets are no longer just speculative retail instruments but are now part of the nation's **long-term financial infrastructure**. This move aligns with broader efforts to modernize fiscal efficiency, improve tax collection, and catch up with global trends in sovereign digital asset management (similar to initiatives seen in hubs like Singapore).

### **5. Most Affected Parties & Impact**
*   **Government (Gains):** Increases fiscal control and efficiency; creates a legal basis for managing state-owned or confiscated crypto assets (estimated at ~$57 million).
*   **Institutional Investors (Gains):** The potential legalization of spot Bitcoin ETFs and clearer custody/licensing rules will likely lower barriers for institutional capital to enter the market via regulated brokerage accounts.
*   **Crypto Exchanges/Businesses (Affected):** Will face stricter compliance, licensing, and capital requirements, but will benefit from greater legal certainty and legitimacy.
*   **Retail Investors (Affected):** Likely to benefit from safer, regulated investment products (ETFs) and improved market stability, though they will continue to operate under a more heavily regulated environment.

### **6. Secondary/Hidden Effects**
*   **"Kimchi Premium" Mitigation:** The move to allow spot ETFs and improve market-making regulations is designed to reduce the price gap between domestic and international crypto markets (the "Kimchi Premium").
*   **Capital Concentration:** The legal framework paves the way for state capital to potentially enter the crypto field, which could accelerate the integration of digital assets with traditional finance.
*   **Tokenization of State Assets:** The government is already planning to tokenize state-owned real estate through Security Token Offerings (STOs) in 2027, which could fundamentally change how public assets are financed and managed.
