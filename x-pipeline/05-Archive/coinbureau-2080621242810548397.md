# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**Status: True.**
On July 23, 2026, the Indian government’s cybercrime agency, the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), issued a formal notice to GitHub ordering the removal of repositories associated with **Bitchat**, a decentralized, Bluetooth-based messaging application developed by Jack Dorsey. Jack Dorsey publicly confirmed the order on X (formerly Twitter) on July 24, 2026, stating: *"The government of india does not like technologies like bitchat and wants it taken down."*

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **The Order:** I4C issued a notice under Section 79(3)(b) of the Information Technology Act, 2000, and Rule 3(1)(d) of the IT Rules, 2021.
*   **Deadline:** GitHub was given a **three-hour window** to disable access to the specified repositories.
*   **The App:** Bitchat is an open-source, peer-to-peer (P2P) messaging app that uses Bluetooth mesh networking. It requires no internet, servers, phone numbers, or user registration.
*   **Trigger Event:** The order followed the observation of protesters associated with the "Cockroach Janata Party" (CJP) using the app to communicate during temporary internet shutdowns in New Delhi (specifically near Jantar Mantar).

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Regulatory Precedent:** This is not the first time Bitchat has faced state-level obstruction. In **April 2026**, China compelled Apple to remove the Bitchat app from its local App Store.
*   **Escalation:** While previous actions (like China's) focused on app store distribution, the Indian government’s move to target the *source code* on GitHub represents an escalation in regulatory strategy, attempting to stifle the project at the development stage.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
*   **Sovereignty vs. Decentralization:** The event highlights a growing global friction between governments seeking to maintain control over information flow (especially during civil unrest) and the rise of "permissionless" technologies designed specifically to be censorship-resistant and infrastructure-independent.
*   **Digital Control Surface:** Governments are increasingly viewing decentralized Bluetooth mesh networking as a "control surface" threat. Unlike traditional platforms (WhatsApp, Signal, SMS) that rely on centralized servers or ISP infrastructure—which can be throttled or blocked—mesh networks are inherently resilient to centralized shutdowns.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Primary Losers:**
    *   **Protesters/Activists:** The primary utility of Bitchat is in high-stakes environments where internet access is restricted. Loss of access to the codebase limits the ability of new users to download and deploy the tool.
    *   **Open-Source Developers:** The move creates a chilling effect on developers building decentralized, privacy-focused communication infrastructure.
*   **Direct Financial Flow:** There is no direct "financial" flow in the sense of a transaction; however, the move represents a "de-risking" of the platform by the intermediary (GitHub) to maintain its legal safe harbor in India. If GitHub refuses, it faces potential legal liability under Indian law.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **The "Streisand Effect":** By attempting to force the removal of the code, the government has drawn significant attention to the app's existence and its effectiveness in bypassing internet shutdowns.
*   **Resilience of Decentralized Code:** Because Bitchat is open-source and has been widely forked (with thousands of copies on GitHub), a takedown request against the primary repository is unlikely to stop its distribution. The code can be hosted on decentralized platforms, mirrors, or distributed via peer-to-peer file sharing, rendering the government's "takedown" largely symbolic in terms of technical efficacy.
