# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### Research Brief: Bitcoin Quantum Recovery Tool

#### 1. Verification Status
**True.** Project Eleven has developed a prototype zero-knowledge (ZK) proof system designed to help users prove ownership of modern Bitcoin wallets (post-2012) in a post-quantum computing environment. The tool is currently unaudited and experimental; it requires changes at the Bitcoin protocol level to be functional on the mainnet. It is confirmed that this tool cannot recover Satoshi Nakamoto's ~1.1 million BTC because those early wallets lack the required hierarchical key structure.

#### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Performance:** The prototype generates a proof in **243 milliseconds** on a standard laptop (M5 MacBook Air) and verifies it in 40 ms.
*   **Core Technology:** Uses a ZK-proof system (built with the Binius system) to prove knowledge of a parent key in a BIP-32 key derivation tree without revealing the key itself.
*   **The "Satoshi" Constraint:** Wallets created before 2012 do not use the BIP-32 hierarchical deterministic (HD) structure, meaning they lack the "parent key" required for this specific recovery method.
*   **Quantum Vulnerability Scale:** Approximately **34% of the total Bitcoin supply** is estimated to be in quantum-vulnerable addresses (addresses where the public key is exposed).
*   **BIP-361 Context:** A proposed Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP-361) would stop new deposits to vulnerable addresses after three years and freeze remaining funds after five years.

#### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Previous Estimates:** Quantum threat analysis previously focused on "Q-Day"—the theoretical point when quantum computers can break elliptic curve cryptography (ECDSA). Recent research (2026) has significantly shortened the timeline for this risk, with some estimates suggesting it could arrive within 5–8 years.
*   **Vulnerability Distribution:** Research indicates that address reuse is a major contributor to quantum risk, accounting for ~4.99 million BTC (roughly 72% of the exposed total), while legacy P2PK (Pay-to-Public-Key) outputs account for ~1.72 million BTC.

#### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The threat of "Q-Day" has moved from theoretical speculation to an engineering reality, prompting government and institutional interest in post-quantum cryptography (PQC). State actors are reportedly "harvesting" encrypted blockchain data today, intending to decrypt it once sufficiently powerful quantum computers become available. This creates a "time-bomb" effect for any data or assets currently secured by standard elliptic curve cryptography.

#### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gains:** Users of modern (post-2012) HD wallets, who now have a potential path to recover assets if they miss migration windows or if their signatures are compromised.
*   **Loses/Affected:** Holders of "zombie" wallets and early adopters (including Satoshi Nakamoto). If BIP-361 or similar protocol-level freezes are implemented, these funds face permanent loss unless a separate recovery mechanism is found.
*   **Financial Flow:** The tool itself does not move money; it creates a framework for future protocol changes. If implemented, it would prevent the "theft" of funds by quantum-equipped attackers and potentially keep those assets under the control of the original owners.

#### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Governance Conflict:** The proposal to freeze "vulnerable" coins (like Satoshi's) has sparked intense debate over Bitcoin’s core value proposition: *immutability* vs. *security*. Forcing a move or freeze risks turning Bitcoin into a "centralized database" managed by a committee, challenging the censorship-resistant nature of the network.
*   **Adoption Pressure:** The development of such tools accelerates the need for a global, coordinated migration to PQC, similar in scale and complexity to Ethereum's "Merge" or the historical block size debates.
