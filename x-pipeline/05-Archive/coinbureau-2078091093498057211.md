# Fact-Checking & Research Brief

### **1. Verification Status**
**Status: True.** The figures cited in the tweet align with reports from July 2026 regarding a significant deleveraging event in the South Korean stock market. Goldman Sachs, specifically trader Ioannis Blekos, provided these statistics in a desk note to clients. The data regarding 1.2 million margin calls and ~350,000 retail account liquidations is consistent with financial news coverage from the week of July 13–17, 2026.

### **2. Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **Margin Calls:** Over 1.2 million leveraged retail accounts hit margin call thresholds (approx. 3% of the country's adult population).
*   **Liquidations:** Approximately 320,000–360,000 retail accounts were forcibly liquidated during the week of July 13, 2026.
*   **Market Impact:** The KOSPI index experienced extreme volatility, including a 6.37% drop on July 16, 2026, following a 6.24% surge the previous day. By mid-July, the index had plunged ~25% from its June 2026 peak.
*   **Monetary Policy:** The Bank of Korea raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to 2.75% on July 16, 2026—the first hike since early 2023—to address market conditions.
*   **Forced Selling:** Total forced liquidations reached 2.3 trillion won over a two-and-a-half-month period leading up to the mid-July crash.

### **3. Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Historical Context:** The 15.4% single-day drop for SK Hynix (July 13) was reported as its largest daily decline in 18 years.
*   **Previous Performance:** Despite the crash, several semiconductor stocks had seen massive year-to-date gains in 2026 prior to the sell-off: SK Hynix (+172%), Samsung (+112%), and Kioxia Holdings (+359%).
*   **Market Expectations:** The rate hike to 2.75% was largely expected by economists (36 out of 37 surveyed by Reuters) but added significant pressure to liquidity-strained retail investors.

### **4. Macro/Geopolitical Context**
The crisis is characterized as a "deleveraging storm" rather than a fundamental economic collapse. It was driven by:
*   **Retail Speculation:** A massive influx of retail "ant investors" using borrowed money and leveraged ETFs to chase the AI and semiconductor boom.
*   **Structural Shifts:** Government policies that historically encouraged moving capital from real estate into equities, combined with high retail participation, created a fragile, highly leveraged market.
*   **Regional Ripple Effects:** The volatility spread to other Asian markets, including Japan (Kioxia, Tokyo Electron) and Taiwan (TSMC), as global risk appetite soured and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) approached bear market territory.

### **5. Most Affected Party & Impact**
*   **Most Affected:** South Korean retail investors ("ants"). Many young investors (aged 20–30) and workers who leveraged savings (including housing funds) were wiped out.
*   **Financial Flow:** Capital flowed out of equities as brokers initiated forced sales to cover margin deficits. This created a "gamma trap" where falling prices triggered further liquidations, accelerating the decline. 
*   **Institutional Role:** While retail investors bore the brunt of the losses, institutional selling and gamma rebalancing from leveraged products acted as an amplifier for the downward momentum.

### **6. Secondary/Hidden Effects**
*   **Social Impact:** The crash has led to significant personal financial distress, with reports of individuals delaying major life events (e.g., weddings) due to the loss of down payments.
*   **Regulatory Reaction:** The Financial Services Commission (FSC) intervened by banning new single-stock leveraged ETF listings and raising margin requirements to curb further speculation.
*   **Crypto/Asset Reclassification:** In a paradoxical move during the market turmoil, the South Korean government introduced the *National Asset Basic Act*, which formally recognizes digital assets as part of state wealth, potentially signaling a long-term shift in how the state manages and views alternative assets.
