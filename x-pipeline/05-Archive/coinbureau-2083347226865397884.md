# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** As of August 1, 2026, the Russian government has officially enacted a ban on cryptocurrency mining in Moscow, the Moscow Region, and parts of the Kursk Region. The ban is scheduled to take effect on **August 15, 2026**, and will remain in force until **December 31, 2032**.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Scope:** The ban applies to Moscow city, the entire Moscow Region, and eight municipal districts in the Kursk Region (including the city of Lgov).
*   **Infrastructure Impact:** Authorities identified at least **65 data centers** operating in the Moscow/Moscow Region, with a combined capacity of **734 megawatts (MW)**.
*   **Reasoning:** The primary driver is "grid strain" and the need to prevent energy capacity shortfalls in power-stressed regions.
*   **Legal Context:** While Russia legalized crypto mining in 2024 (effective Nov 1, 2024), this new measure uses a "geographically targeted" approach to mitigate infrastructure overloads.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Total Mining Capacity:** The 734 MW figure represents the specific load identified by the Ministry of Energy in the Moscow area.
*   **Previous Restrictions:** Prior to this, Russia had already implemented region-specific bans in the Republic of Buryatia, the Trans-Baikal Territory, and parts of the North Caucasus (Dagestan, Ingushetia, etc.), reflecting an ongoing trend of tightening regional controls since early 2025.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** In late 2024, Russia began imposing regional mining prohibitions (e.g., in the North Caucasus and Buryatia). These were the first major instances of the government using its legal framework to actively "prune" mining density in grid-stressed areas.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** The "registry-based" approach. The 2024 legalization law required miners to join an FNS (Federal Tax Service) registry. By 2026, authorities used this registry data to pinpoint the 65 data centers and enforce targeted shutdowns rather than issuing a blanket national ban.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** The Russian Ministry of Energy has consistently prioritized "energy sovereignty" over crypto industry growth. Despite the 2024 legalization, Deputy Energy Minister Yevgeny Grabchak has been a vocal proponent of using regional bans to protect industrial and residential power supplies.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
This is a shift from "legalization for economic opportunity" to "energy-constrained regulation." Following sanctions, Russia initially viewed crypto as a tool for cross-border trade (bypassing SWIFT). However, the reality of aging energy infrastructure and the impact of the war (particularly in border regions like Kursk) has forced the state to prioritize domestic grid stability over the proliferation of decentralized mining operations.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Losers:** Industrial miners in the Moscow power zone are the most affected. They face immediate capital flight or forced relocation to energy-surplus regions (e.g., Siberia).
*   **Gains:** The Russian state-owned power grid operator and industrial/residential consumers in these regions, who will benefit from the freed-up 734 MW of capacity.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Regulatory Arbitrage:** The ban will likely trigger a massive, internal migration of mining equipment from the Central Federal District to Russia's eastern regions (Siberia), where power is cheaper and more abundant, potentially leading to a "geographic concentration" of the industry.
*   **Criminalization Risks:** With the State Duma advancing bills to jail unregistered miners (fines up to 2.5 million rubles and prison terms up to 5 years), the "gray market" for mining is rapidly shrinking, forcing remaining operators to either fully comply or go underground.
