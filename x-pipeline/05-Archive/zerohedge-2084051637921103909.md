# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** On August 3, 2026, the Japanese Ministry of Finance (MOF) officially confirmed that it conducted a coordinated yen-buying intervention with the U.S. Department of the Treasury on Friday, July 31, 2026 (U.S. Eastern Time). This action was taken to counter "excessive volatility and disorderly movements" in the Japanese yen, which had recently hit 40-year lows near 164 JPY/USD.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Event Date:** July 31, 2026 (U.S. ET).
*   **Nature of Action:** Coordinated yen-buying intervention.
*   **Legal/Policy Basis:** U.S.-Japan Finance Ministers' Joint Statement (September 2025).
*   **Scale:** While specific intervention totals for July 31 are not yet fully disclosed, market sources and BOJ data suggested substantial activity, with reports of up to $58.97 billion sold to support the yen during the broader period around the decision.
*   **Context:** First joint U.S.-Japan FX intervention since March 2011.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Prior Intervention (April–May 2026):** Japan previously executed a massive solo campaign, spending approximately ¥11.73 trillion (~$73.5 billion) to arrest yen depreciation.
*   **Historical Lows:** The yen had been languishing in the 163–164 range, levels not seen since 1986.
*   **Market Impact:** Following the July 31 intervention, the USD/JPY pair saw a sharp reversal, with the yen jumping significantly from the 164 levels toward 157–158.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** The last joint U.S.-Japan intervention occurred in **March 2011** (post-Great East Japan Earthquake), aimed at stabilizing markets.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** The use of the **FIMA (Foreign and International Monetary Authorities) Repo Facility** is being cited as a structural tool to provide temporary dollar liquidity, signaling a shift toward more durable intervention frameworks rather than just one-off market operations.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** The September 2025 Joint Statement established a formal "playbook" for coordination, explicitly allowing for intervention against "disorderly" moves, which has been the guiding doctrine for Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama’s tenure.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The intervention highlights the extreme pressure created by the **widening interest rate differential** between the Bank of Japan (policy rate ~0.75%) and the Federal Reserve (maintaining a significantly higher restrictive policy). The move is seen as a strategic alignment to prevent disorderly markets that could spill over into U.S. bond market instability.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gainers:** Holders of JPY-denominated assets and Japanese importers (benefit from a stronger yen lowering costs of energy/food imports).
*   **Losers:** Carry traders who were shorting the yen to fund high-yield dollar assets.
*   **Financial Flow:** The intervention involves selling U.S. Dollar reserves to buy Japanese Yen, directly tightening dollar liquidity in the market and forcing a repricing of JPY short positions.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Corporate Hedging:** Increased volatility is pushing Japanese firms to pivot toward alternative hedges, including Bitcoin, to manage currency risk.
*   **Policy Constraints:** The intervention may limit Japan's "fiscal space" for future stimulus, as excessive spending on currency defense drains liquid reserves that might otherwise be used for domestic fiscal policy.
