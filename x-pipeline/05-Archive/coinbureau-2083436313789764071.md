# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** Multiple reports confirm that on Friday, July 31, 2026, the U.S. Treasury intervened in currency markets to support the Japanese Yen (JPY) by selling euros and purchasing yen. This action followed Japan's own intervention efforts. The intervention was corroborated by reports of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s "to-do" list, which explicitly mentioned purchasing "$5-10 billion" in Japanese Yen.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Action:** The Federal Reserve Bank of New York, acting on behalf of the U.S. Treasury, sold euros to buy yen.
*   **Execution Agents:** Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley were reported as the banks executing the trades.
*   **Target Magnitude:** A notepad belonging to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent indicated a target purchase of **$5 billion to $10 billion** in JPY.
*   **Market Context:** The yen had recently hit its weakest level since 1986 (or near 40-year lows).
*   **Market Reaction:** The dollar dropped from approximately 158.9 JPY to 157.6 JPY during Friday afternoon trading.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Historical Context:** This is the first time the U.S. has directly intervened to support the yen in decades. Reports vary on the "nearly 30 years" figure, with some noting it as the first such joint effort in ~30 years and others citing 2011 as the last instance of U.S. support for the yen (during the G7 coordinated action post-tsunami).

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** The 2011 G7 coordinated intervention is the primary recent benchmark for U.S. involvement in yen stabilization. In that event, the U.S. joined other G7 nations to stabilize markets after the Great East Japan Earthquake and tsunami.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** Japan's own intervention strategy frequently involves massive direct market liquidity injection. For instance, in October 2022, Japan spent a record amount (estimated over $50 billion) on yen-buying interventions.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s public "to-do list" exposure is a rare, high-profile example of "open-book" policy signaling, which functions as a psychological tool to influence market participants without necessarily needing to exhaust the full $5–10 billion budget.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
The intervention signals a shift in U.S. policy toward addressing the extreme volatility caused by the interest rate differential between the U.S. and Japan. The move is designed to curb speculative attacks on the yen, which have been exacerbated by rising import costs and inflation in Japan.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gains:** The Japanese government and the Bank of Japan, as the intervention provides relief to the yen and tempers import inflation.
*   **Loses/Affected:** Carry-trade speculators who were shorting the yen against the dollar/euro. The direct financial flow involves the U.S. Treasury effectively draining euro liquidity to bolster JPY demand, putting upward pressure on the yen’s value.

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Euro Volatility:** Because the U.S. sold euros to buy yen, the intervention creates secondary pressure on the EUR/USD exchange rate.
*   **Signaling Effect:** The mere "threat" or signal of intervention (the "rate check" and the notepad) often has a larger impact on volatility than the actual dollar amount spent, as it forces market makers to re-price risk and exit short positions.
