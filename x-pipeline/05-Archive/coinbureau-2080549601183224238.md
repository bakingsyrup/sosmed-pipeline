# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** Poolin Technology PTE. Ltd., along with its U.S. affiliates (Lonestar Dream Inc. and Lonestar Taproot LLC), filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey on July 22, 2026. The filing confirms the company is liquidating its remaining assets, including its West Texas mining sites, rather than attempting a corporate reorganization.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Liabilities:** Approximately **$173.1 million** in total prepetition obligations.
*   **Customer Debt:** Roughly **$163.7 million** in unsecured IOUs owed to approximately **11,700** former wallet customers (stemming from a 2022 withdrawal freeze).
*   **Assets:** Listed in the range of **$1 million to $10 million**.
*   **Proposed Asset Sale:** A **$52 million** "stalking horse" bid from Thor CALAP LLC for two West Texas mining sites (Pyote: $15M; Tarbush: $37M).
*   **Historical Peak:** In 2019, Poolin was the world's largest Bitcoin mining pool, at times controlling nearly 20% of the global hashrate. It currently holds roughly 0.2% market share.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **2022 Crisis:** Poolin suspended withdrawals in September 2022, transitioning from a top-tier mining pool to a distressed entity.
*   **Operational Deficits:** The company incurred cumulative operating deficits of approximately **$45.9 million** in Texas before ceasing operations at its sites on July 10, 2026.
*   **Recovery Outlook:** The $52 million opening bid represents only about **31.8%** of the total outstanding wallet IOUs, excluding bankruptcy administrative costs and potential creditor priority issues.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
Poolin’s collapse is a multi-year "slow-motion" failure. It was heavily impacted by China’s 2021 crackdown on crypto mining, which forced a chaotic, capital-intensive migration to the U.S. The firm's subsequent pivot into custodial-style financial products (USDT lending/deposits) proved fatal when the 2022 "crypto winter" created a liquidity mismatch. The current bankruptcy is part of a broader trend where mining firms are either liquidating or pivoting assets toward AI and high-performance computing (HPC) data centers to survive.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Primary Losers:** The **11,700 retail/unsecured wallet customers** are the most affected. They have been waiting since 2022 for access to their funds. Because their claims are unsecured, they face significant uncertainty regarding recovery, as the $52 million sale price is a "floor" and proceeds must first navigate bankruptcy legal fees, liens, and competing creditor claims.
*   **Financial Flow:** Capital is being extracted from the liquidation of industrial infrastructure (power rights and mining equipment) to pay off unsecured debt, effectively transferring the residual value of the company’s physical assets to its historical creditors.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **AI Infrastructure Pivot:** The bankruptcy highlights the "gold rush" for power-rich sites; the auction is explicitly targeting AI and HPC developers, confirming that Bitcoin mining sites are now highly sought-after utility assets for the AI tech stack.
*   **Creditor Fatigue:** This case serves as a reminder of the long "tail" of the 2022 crypto collapse, where thousands of users remain trapped in bankruptcy proceedings years after the initial liquidity events.
