# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** As of July 15, 2026, BlackRock officially reported that its Assets Under Management (AUM) reached **$15.34 trillion** for the second quarter of 2026. This confirms the firm has surpassed the $15 trillion milestone, making it the first investment firm in history to manage a portfolio of this magnitude.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers (Q2 2026)
*   **Total AUM:** $15.34 trillion.
*   **Quarterly Net Inflows:** $191.7 billion.
*   **Revenue:** $7.08 billion (a 31% increase year-over-year).
*   **Adjusted EPS:** $13.91 (exceeding analyst estimates of ~$12.57).
*   **Operating Margin:** 45.9% (highest in nearly five years).
*   **Contextual Comparison:** This figure is equivalent to approximately half of the U.S. GDP and exceeds the GDP of every country in the world except for the United States and China.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Previous Quarter (Q1 2026):** ~$13.89 trillion.
*   **Year-over-Year (Q2 2025):** ~$12.53 trillion.
*   **Historical Growth:** The firm crossed $12 trillion in mid-2025 and $14 trillion by late 2025, demonstrating an accelerating growth trajectory.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
BlackRock's dominance is driven by its role as a "super-manager" that sits at the intersection of public markets, private credit, and financial technology (Aladdin). The surge in AUM is fueled by a rally in global equity markets, which contributed significantly to asset appreciation, combined with sustained demand for ETFs (including Bitcoin/Ether ETFs) and private market strategies.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gains:** BlackRock shareholders (saw shares jump ~6–7% on the news) and the firm’s executive team. The firm also announced increased share buybacks (to $550 million/quarter) and dividends.
*   **Market Impact:** As the largest shareholder in a vast majority of S&P 500 companies, this concentration of capital gives BlackRock unprecedented influence over corporate governance, proxy voting, and capital allocation across the global economy.
*   **Financial Flow:** Retail and institutional capital continues to flow heavily into passive investment vehicles (ETFs), effectively centralizing the "ownership" of the global corporate sector into a few massive index fund managers (BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street).

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Systemic Concentration Risk:** The sheer scale of $15 trillion managed on a single risk-management platform (Aladdin) creates "too big to fail" dynamics, where a technical or market failure at BlackRock could trigger widespread systemic instability.
*   **Antitrust Scrutiny:** The milestone will likely reignite debates in Washington and abroad regarding the "Big Three" (BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street) and their effective control over the broader market, potentially leading to renewed calls for stricter regulation or legislative efforts to limit their market share.
*   **Market Distortion:** With such massive flows, BlackRock’s rebalancing and index-tracking activities can move markets independently of underlying company fundamentals, potentially creating artificial price floors or volatility during large-scale portfolio adjustments.
