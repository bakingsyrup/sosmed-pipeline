# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**True.** As of August 3, 2026, Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) has confirmed the completion of its third Bitcoin sale of 2026. The company sold 1,638 BTC between July 27 and August 2 for approximately $104.7 million. The proceeds were used to bolster its USD cash reserve (now $4 billion) and repurchase $81 million of its STRC preferred shares.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **BTC Sold:** 1,638 BTC.
*   **Sale Value:** ~$104.7 million.
*   **Average Sale Price:** $63,957 per BTC.
*   **Current BTC Treasury:** 842,138 BTC (as of August 2, 2026).
*   **USD Reserve:** $4 billion.
*   **Capital Allocation:** $52.4 million for preferred stock dividends; $52.3 million for STRC share repurchases.
*   **Context:** This follows a larger sale of 3,588 BTC in early July 2026.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Prior Period:** For years, Michael Saylor maintained a "never sell" policy. The shift began in May 2026 with a small sale of 32 BTC, followed by a major sale in July 2026 (3,588 BTC for ~$216 million).
*   **Market Expectation:** Markets previously expected Strategy to remain a perpetual net buyer. The new "Digital Credit Capital Framework" (adopted June 29, 2026) signaled a pivot toward active capital management, including using BTC sales to fund dividends and buybacks.

### 4. Historical Precedents & Past Benchmarks
*   **Category A (Historical Event Anchor):** The July 2026 sale of 3,588 BTC for $216 million was the first significant net reduction since the company began its Bitcoin treasury strategy in 2020.
*   **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook):** Corporate treasury departments typically shift from "growth/accumulation" to "liquidity management" when fixed-income obligations (like preferred stock dividends) exceed organic cash flow. Strategy's 12% dividend on STRC shares necessitated this shift to prevent further equity dilution.
*   **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor):** Saylor historically utilized equity issuance (issuing MSTR shares to buy BTC) to grow the treasury. The current shift demonstrates a reversal: using BTC assets to support the equity/credit structure when the "flywheel" (issuing stock at a premium to NAV) became less efficient due to market discounts.

### 5. Macro/Geopolitical Context
Strategy is transitioning from a "Bitcoin-only" vehicle to a complex "Bitcoin-backed financial holding company." By managing a $4 billion USD reserve alongside its BTC holdings, the firm is attempting to immunize itself against volatility that could threaten its high-yield preferred stock obligations, effectively treating BTC as a reserve asset rather than a static store-of-value.

### 6. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gains:** Preferred shareholders (STRC) benefit from the repurchase program and the guaranteed funding of dividends.
*   **Loses/Affected:** Long-term "Bitcoin Maximalist" investors who bought MSTR specifically for its "never sell", pure-exposure profile.
*   **Financial Flow:** Capital is flowing from liquidated Bitcoin into cash reserves and back into the company’s own capital structure (share buybacks/dividends).

### 7. Secondary/Hidden Effects
The shift creates a "liquidity feedback loop": if the company must sell BTC to pay dividends, and the market perceives this as a bearish signal, MSTR stock may trade at a wider discount to its Net Asset Value (NAV), making it harder to issue new equity, which in turn forces more BTC sales to cover cash needs.
