### Research Brief: Q2 2024 Bank Earnings Analysis

The tweet in question references the Q2 2024 earnings season for major U.S. banks (JPMorgan Chase, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Goldman Sachs, and Morgan Stanley).

---

#### 1. Verification Status
**True.** The data reflects the official Q2 2024 earnings reports released in mid-July 2024. The narrative surrounding these reports—that banks are navigating a "higher-for-longer" interest rate environment while preparing for potential credit deterioration—is accurate.

#### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Net Interest Income (NII) Pressure:** Most banks reported a decline or stagnation in NII as funding costs (interest paid on deposits) rose faster than loan yields.
    *   **JPMorgan Chase:** NII came in at $22.9 billion, missing analyst expectations slightly.
    *   **Bank of America:** NII dropped 3% year-over-year to $13.7 billion.
*   **Provision for Credit Losses (PCL):** Banks are setting aside more cash for "rainy days."
    *   **Citigroup:** Increased reserves significantly due to credit card delinquency normalization.
    *   **Wells Fargo:** Reported a decline in NII, projecting a 7-9% drop for the full year.
*   **Investment Banking Recovery:** A bright spot. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley saw a rebound in underwriting and M&A advisory fees, signaling a thaw in corporate deal-making.

#### 3. Macro/Geopolitical Context
We are in a **"Normalization Phase."** During the zero-interest-rate policy (ZIRP) era, banks made easy money. Now, they are caught in a squeeze: 
*   **The Yield Curve:** The inverted yield curve (short-term rates higher than long-term) is historically toxic for bank profitability, as they borrow short and lend long.
*   **The "Soft Landing" Bet:** Banks are betting that the economy won't crash, but they are terrified of the "lag effect" of high rates finally hitting the consumer’s ability to pay back debt.

#### 4. Impact on Retail ("Kita Retail")
*   **Who Gains:** 
    *   **Savers (to an extent):** Finally seeing higher yields on High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs) and CDs, though banks are aggressively cutting these rates as the Fed signals cuts.
    *   **Shareholders:** Despite the NII squeeze, most banks maintained or increased dividends and share buybacks, rewarding long-term holders.
*   **Who gets REKT:**
    *   **Credit Card Borrowers:** Banks are reporting higher delinquency rates. Retail consumers carrying debt are being crushed by double-digit APRs.
    *   **Small Business Borrowers:** High rates are making capital expansion prohibitively expensive, leading to stagnation in the "real economy" outside of the tech/AI bubble.

#### 5. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **The "Deposit Flight" Risk:** As banks lower deposit rates to protect their own margins (NII), retail depositors are moving money into Money Market Funds (MMFs) and Treasury bonds. This drains liquidity from the banking system, forcing banks to rely on more expensive wholesale funding.
*   **The "Credit Crunch" by Stealth:** Because banks are increasing reserves for bad loans, they are becoming pickier about who they lend to. This is a "stealth tightening" of credit. You don't see a headline saying "Banks stopped lending," but you will notice it's much harder to get a small business loan or an auto loan, even if your credit score is decent.
*   **AI/Tech Spending vs. Real Economy:** Banks are spending billions on AI infrastructure to cut labor costs. This keeps their stock prices high (benefiting institutional investors) while potentially signaling a long-term reduction in banking sector employment.

**Bottom Line:** The banks are signaling that the "free money" era is dead. They are shifting from growth-at-any-cost to capital preservation, which effectively means tighter credit for the average person and a higher "toll" on the economy.
