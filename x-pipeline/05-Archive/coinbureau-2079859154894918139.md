# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### Verification Status
**True.** Movement Labs (the core developer of the Movement blockchain) filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on July 15, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-11113). The filing confirms the project's severe financial distress following a series of governance disputes, a market-making scandal, and failed strategic pivots.

### Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Financial Standing:** The company listed assets of **$100,001–$500,000** and liabilities of up to **$10 million**.
*   **Fundraising:** The project had raised approximately **$41.4 million** in equity financing (including a $38M Series A) and was once valued at roughly **$3 billion** in early 2025.
*   **Revenue & Fees:** On-chain data confirms daily app revenue has remained below **$800** since November 2025, with chain fees dropping to as low as **$1** in the 24 hours preceding the news.
*   **Token Performance:** The MOVE token is down over **97–99%** from its all-time high (ATH) reached in December 2024.
*   **Creditors:** The filing lists nearly 300 creditors, with the largest unsecured claim (over $1.6 million) held by ousted co-founder Rushi Manche.

### Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Market Context:** The divergence between the $41.4M–$141M+ (total capital raised) and the near-zero revenue generation is cited as a hallmark of "zombie" infrastructure projects—those that secure high venture valuations based on technical promises (Move language integration) but fail to achieve product-market fit or sustained user activity.
*   **Operational Continuity:** While Movement Labs (the dev company) is in bankruptcy, Move Industries—which took over ecosystem operations in December 2025—claims to remain operational, highlighting a fragmented corporate structure common in distressed crypto projects.

### Macro/Geopolitical Context
This event reflects the broader "funding overhang" in the crypto sector, where projects that raised massive capital during the 2024–2025 cycle are now facing a "liquidity reality check." The failure is specifically linked to the collapse of the Diem-derived "Move" language narrative and the inability of high-valuation L2s to compete with established networks once the speculative incentive phase (point farming) ends.

### Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Losers:** 
    *   **Retail Investors:** Those who purchased MOVE tokens post-launch have suffered near-total losses.
    *   **Venture Capitalists:** Firms like Polychain Capital, which led Series A rounds, face significant write-downs on their equity stakes in the development company.
    *   **Creditors:** Vendors and service providers (including legal counsel and custodians like Anchorage Digital) are now competing for limited assets in a bankruptcy process where liabilities dwarf available capital.
*   **Direct Financial Flow:** Capital flowed from VC firms to the development company, which was then largely depleted by operational burn and legal costs, rather than being returned to the ecosystem via sustainable network utility.

### Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Legal Precedent:** The bankruptcy highlights the risk of "toxic" market-making agreements, where intermediaries are granted large portions of token supply. The legal battle involving the ousted co-founder over DOJ-related legal fees sets a precedent for how internal governance disputes can accelerate the insolvency of crypto startups.
*   **Ecosystem "Zombie" Risk:** The separation of "Movement Labs" (bankrupt) from "Move Industries" (operational) creates a confusing landscape for users, potentially leading to "jurisdiction shopping" or entity-splitting tactics to avoid total project shutdown, even when the underlying network lacks economic viability.
