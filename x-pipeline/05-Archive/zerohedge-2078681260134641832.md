### 1. Verification Status
**Status: True (in the context of ongoing 2026 regional conflicts).**
Reports from multiple intelligence and defense analysis outlets confirm that Iran has transitioned its military navigation and targeting systems from the U.S.-controlled Global Positioning System (GPS) to China’s BeiDou Navigation Satellite System (BDS-3). This shift follows the June 2025 conflict between Iran, Israel, and the U.S., where Iranian assets faced significant disruption due to GPS jamming and spoofing. By 2026, the integration of BeiDou has been identified by analysts as a key factor in the increased precision of Iranian missile and drone strikes.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **System Shift:** Iran moved from GPS to China’s **BDS-3 (BeiDou-3)**.
*   **Technical Advantage:** BeiDou-3 offers military-tier signals (B3A) that utilize complex frequency hopping and Navigation Message Authentication (NMA) to resist jamming and spoofing.
*   **Performance:** Analysts report a **98% positioning success rate** for BDS-equipped hardware and a **Circular Error Probability (CEP) of under five meters** for missile strikes.
*   **Strategic Context:** The transition is supported by the 25-year strategic partnership signed between China and Iran in 2021.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Pre-2026:** During the 12-day war in June 2025, Iranian drones and missiles relied heavily on U.S. GPS, which was effectively neutralized by Israeli and U.S. electronic warfare (EW) systems, leading to high failure rates for Iranian strike packages.
*   **System Maturity:** China completed the global deployment of the BeiDou-3 system in 2020, making it a viable, mature alternative to GPS, GLONASS (Russia), and Galileo (EU).

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
This event marks a critical rupture in Western technological hegemony over global navigation infrastructure. It signals a move toward a "bipolar" or "multipolar" satellite navigation architecture where regional powers can bypass Western-controlled digital infrastructure. For China, it serves as a method to project power and support allies without direct military intervention, while Iran achieves "digital sovereignty," insulating its military and critical logistics from Western signal denial.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Most Affected:** **Israel and the United States.** Their ability to use electronic warfare to disrupt Iranian drone and missile swarms has been significantly degraded.
*   **Financial/Strategic Flow:** The "cost" is a loss of military deterrence for the U.S.-Israel alliance. Iran gains a decisive tactical advantage in strike precision, while China gains a massive data-gathering and geopolitical foothold in the Middle East, effectively turning Iran into a "laboratory" for testing anti-Western military technologies.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Global South Adoption:** If the effectiveness of BeiDou is confirmed in live combat, other nations currently reliant on GPS may begin diversifying their navigation infrastructure, leading to a fragmented global PNT (Positioning, Navigation, and Timing) landscape.
*   **Increased Escalation:** The ability to conduct precision strikes with high confidence may embolden Iranian strike doctrine, potentially leading to more frequent or daring military engagements, as the "Achilles' heel" of navigation vulnerability has been largely mitigated.
