# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### Verification Status
**True.** On July 22, 2026, the U.S. House of Representatives passed the **Stop Insider Trading Act (H.R. 7008)** in a **232–198 vote**. The vote was largely along party lines, with only 13 Democrats joining the Republican majority. Many Democrats opposed the bill, labeling it a "sham" due to the inclusion of a "poison pill" voter identification requirement and the exclusion of the President and Vice President from the trading restrictions.

### Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Vote Count:** 232 (Yes) – 198 (No).
*   **Bill Provisions:** 
    *   Prohibits Members of Congress, their spouses, and dependent children from purchasing individual stocks.
    *   Requires public disclosure at least seven days *prior* to the sale of existing holdings.
    *   **Exclusions:** Does not require divestiture of current assets. Does not apply to the President or Vice President.
    *   **Added Provision:** Includes a requirement for photo identification for voters in federal elections.
*   **Penalties:** $2,000 or 10% of the transaction value (whichever is greater), plus forfeiture of realized gains.

### Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Previous Legislation:** The **STOCK Act (2012)** currently governs congressional ethics regarding insider trading, prohibiting the use of non-public information for private profit. Unlike the new bill, the STOCK Act focuses on disclosure and enforcement rather than a prospective purchase ban.
*   **Market Context:** Congressional stock trading has been a recurring point of contention, with various bipartisan proposals in recent years aiming for broader divestiture requirements that were not included in this iteration.

### Macro/Geopolitical Context
This vote is a calculated political maneuver ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. By bundling a popular ethics reform (stock trading ban) with a controversial election integrity measure (voter ID), Republicans aimed to force Democrats into a difficult position: either support a "poison pill" or vote against a bill that purports to curb corruption. The inclusion of the President's exemption further highlights the partisan divide over executive vs. legislative accountability.

### Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gainers:** Republican leadership gains a campaign talking point regarding both ethics reform and election security.
*   **Losers:** Retail investors and the public trust in Congress may suffer if the bill is viewed as ineffective "political theater" rather than substantive reform.
*   **Financial Flow:** No direct capital flow is triggered, but the bill limits the future investment flexibility of lawmakers and their families, potentially forcing them to shift assets into blind trusts or broad index funds if they wish to avoid the new, stricter disclosure and penalty regime.

### Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Senate Standoff:** The inclusion of the voter ID provision makes the bill’s passage in the Senate highly uncertain, likely resulting in the bill dying in the upper chamber.
*   **Divestiture Loophole:** Because the bill allows for the retention of current holdings, it fails to address the "conflict of interest" argument regarding assets already held by members, arguably rendering the "insider trading" prevention incomplete.
