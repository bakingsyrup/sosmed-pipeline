# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### **Verification Status**
**True.** The Russian State Duma is scheduled to hold the second and third readings of the comprehensive cryptocurrency regulation bill, titled "On Digital Currency and Digital Rights" (Draft Bill No. 1194918-8), on **Tuesday, July 21, 2026**. While the tweet refers to a "final vote," the legislative process requires subsequent approval by the Federation Council and the President's signature before becoming law.

### **Key Facts & Numbers**
*   **Core Provisions**: The bill classifies cryptocurrency as "property" but maintains a strict ban on its use for domestic payments within Russia.
*   **International Trade**: It provides a legal framework for companies to use cryptocurrencies for cross-border settlements, a move intended to bypass Western banking sanctions and SWIFT restrictions.
*   **Retail Investment Limits**:
    *   **Non-Qualified Investors**: Annual purchase cap of 300,000 rubles (~$3,800) through a single intermediary, with a 100,000 ruble limit for foreign transfers.
    *   **Qualified Investors**: Higher caps of 3 million rubles for purchases and 1 million rubles for foreign transfers.
*   **Timeline**: If passed, major provisions are expected to take effect on **September 1, 2026**. Unlicensed platforms may face bans starting in July 2027.

### **Comparative Baseline Data**
*   **Previous Status**: The bill passed its first reading in April 2024 (or early 2026, per reports) with strong support (327 out of 340 deputies).
*   **Previous Framework**: Prior to this, crypto activity existed in a "gray zone" or under limited experimental legal regimes (EPR) that struggled to gain wide application due to the lack of clear legal definitions.

### **Macro/Geopolitical Context**
This legislation is a strategic pivot to mitigate the impact of international sanctions. By creating a state-controlled, legal pathway for cross-border crypto settlements, Russia aims to maintain trade flows with partners (e.g., China, Turkey) despite its exclusion from traditional Western-dominated financial infrastructure.

### **Most Affected Party & Impact**
*   **Beneficiaries**: Russian export/import businesses that have faced severe friction in cross-border payments due to sanctions.
*   **Regulated Entities**: Licensed exchanges and intermediaries will gain legal standing but must adhere to strict Bank of Russia oversight, including identity verification and transaction monitoring.
*   **Retail Investors**: Face both "legalization" (protection/formal status) and "restriction" (caps on exposure), shifting from an unregulated environment to a highly controlled, tiered system.

### **Secondary/Hidden Effects**
*   **State Control**: The move is not a liberalization of the crypto market but an attempt to bring it into a state-controlled system.
*   **Compliance Costs**: The requirement for licensed platforms to act as tax agents and the potential for 48-hour holds on certain foreign transfers will likely increase operational friction for users.
*   **Global Signal**: The formalization of crypto for trade by a G20 economy may influence the regulatory calculus in other sanctioned or emerging-market nations looking to reduce reliance on the U.S. dollar.
