# Fact-Checking & Research Brief
### 1. Verification Status
**Status: Verified.**
On July 17, 2026, Nigerian President Bola Tinubu signed the **Presidential Executive Order on Virtual Assets Coordination, 2026**, which took immediate effect. The order establishes a **Virtual Asset Council (VAC)** to harmonize the regulatory oversight of digital assets across various government agencies.

### 2. Key Facts & Numbers
*   **Council Structure:** The Virtual Asset Council (VAC) is chaired by the **Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)**. Vice-chairs include the **Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)** and the **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)**. Other members include the **Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU)** and the **Office of the National Security Adviser (ONSA)**.
*   **Timeline:** The Council has **30 days** from the signing date (by mid-August 2026) to produce a harmonized implementation framework.
*   **Regulatory Logic:** The order does not create a new regulator but coordinates existing ones. The SEC retains oversight of virtual assets classified as securities, while the CBN handles non-securities (payments, transfers, custody).
*   **New Initiatives:** The order mandates the creation of a **regulatory sandbox** for testing products and a dedicated tax policy for the virtual assets sector.

### 3. Comparative Baseline Data
*   **Previous Environment:** The landscape was previously fragmented, with regulators like the SEC and CBN often operating in silos. This led to "regulatory grey zones" following the 2021 banking restrictions (which were later reversed in 2023).
*   **Market Context:** Nigeria consistently ranks as one of the world's leaders in cryptocurrency adoption by transaction volume. The new order follows the *Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025*, which formally classified digital assets as securities.

### 4. Macro/Geopolitical Context
Nigeria is attempting to transition from a "restrict-and-ignore" approach to a "regulate-and-capture" model. By formalizing the sector, the government aims to:
*   **Capture Revenue:** The involvement of the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) signals a push to tax the massive, often informal, crypto trading volume.
*   **National Security:** By including the ONSA and NFIU, the government is prioritizing the mitigation of money laundering and terrorism financing, which are often cited as reasons for strict oversight in emerging markets.

### 5. Most Affected Party & Impact
*   **Gainers:** Institutional players and legitimate fintech startups benefit from the "regulatory sandbox" and clearer, predictable rules, which reduce the risk of sudden operational shutdowns.
*   **Losers/Most Affected:** Unregistered/informal operators and "grey-market" exchanges that thrived in the absence of coordination. These entities will face increased scrutiny and potential forced registration or exit.
*   **Financial Flow:** The flow shifts from unmonitored peer-to-peer (P2P) and offshore channels toward regulated domestic infrastructure, allowing the state to impose tax levies and monitor systemic risk.

### 6. Secondary/Hidden Effects
*   **Compliance Squeeze:** While the framework aims to "harmonize," the 30-day deadline for an implementation framework suggests a rapid tightening of compliance requirements. Smaller firms may lack the capital to meet these new standards, leading to market consolidation.
*   **Policy Durability:** The success of this initiative hinges on whether the CBN and SEC can actually resolve their historical turf wars within the new Council structure.
