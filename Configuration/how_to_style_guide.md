# 📌 Dissection: Generalized How-To & Guides (Miles Deutscher Style)

This style guide governs the generation of X Teaser Posts and Linked X Articles. The agent must evaluate the input pitch to determine whether it requires a **Tactical Builder (How-To)** layout or a **Strategic Research (Guide)** layout.

---

## 🧠 Structural Analysis

### 1. Hook Post Formula (Teaser X Post)
The teaser post must be exactly three blocks of text separated by double line breaks. The agent must craft custom copy for each block rather than repeating template keywords:
* **Line 1 (The Hook):** An attention-grabbing, high-stakes claim or a curiosity-gap statement customized to the pitch.
* **Line 2 (Credibility / Value Setup):** A personal validation sentence establishing skin-in-the-game ("I spent the last month...", "I built...") and framing the value for the reader.
* **Line 3 (Outcome / CTA):** A custom transition summarizing the pillars, plays, or action items of the guide, ending strictly with a colon (`:`) to attach the article card below.

---

### 2. Article Structure Flow (Long-Form)

> [!NOTE]
> All long-form articles must follow one of the two templates below depending on the target topic. Avoid generic H2 headers; customize them using active verbs and terms specific to the topic.

#### 🏛️ Variant A: Tactical Builder (Step-by-Step Tutorial)
*Use this mode for tool configurations, setup guides, prompts, workflows, and developer tutorials.*
* **H1 Title:** A benefit-driven title focusing on action, speed, or system capabilities.
* **Intro:** A 1-to-2 paragraph opening establishing creator authority, the personal effort spent coding/testing, and a bold promise of time or cost savings.
* **Outline:** A Table of Contents block linking to the custom H2 headers.
* **Concept Explanation:** Contextual overview explaining why this workflow/system matters.
* **Tactical Steps:** Chronological steps formatted as H2 headers starting with `Step X: [Active Verb + Target]` (e.g. *Step 1: Initializing the API*, *Step 2: Structuring Your Prompts*). Provide copy-pasteable prompts, scripts, or configurations under each step.
* **Plays / Monetization:** 2-3 custom plays detailing how the reader can use this tool to capture revenue or save hours.
* **TL;DR & Risks:** Actionable safety advice warning the reader to test in sandbox/paper environments first.
* **CTA:** A custom download/save call to action directing readers to a cheatsheet or templates, followed by social handles.

#### 📊 Variant B: Strategic Research (Narrative & Investment Guide)
*Use this mode for ecosystem deep-dives, market trends, narratives, and positioning reports.*
* **H1 Title:** A high-impact title focusing on the thesis, narrative name, and opportunities.
* **Intro:** A 1-to-2 paragraph opening addressing current noise/hype, detailing personal deep-dive research, and framing the opportunity.
* **Outline:** A Table of Contents block linking to the custom H2 headers.
* **Concept Breakdown:** Plain explanation of the trend/ecosystem and its core drivers.
* **Quantitative Proof:** H2 section backing up the trend using raw data, TVL metrics, DEX volumes, active user counts, or speed statistics.
* **Direct Plays:** H2 section outlining 2-3 specific projects, coins, or actions to gain exposure, detailing exact entry triggers or support ranges.
* **Second-Order Impacts:** H2 section explaining indirect beneficiaries or infrastructure assets that benefit as a tailwind.
* **TL;DR (Recap) & Risks:** Bulleted recap of positioning and risk warnings (e.g. smart contract vulnerability, vaporware, or asset drawdowns).
* **CTA:** Calls to action directing readers to deep-dive newsletters or reports, followed by social handles.

## ⚡ Core Linguistic, Voice & Copywriting Rules

### 1. Voice, Tone & Persona (The Pragmatic Analyst)
*   **The Persona:** A pragmatic, street-smart macroeconomist and tech analyst. Writes as a knowledgeable trading peer talking to other investors.
*   **High-Conviction Alpha:** Writes with strong, authoritative declarations ("*The opportunity here is real*", "*If you only pay attention to one thing...*").
*   **Opt-In Risk Awareness:** Transparent about risk but frames it as part of the game ("*Everything here can go to zero*", "*NFA*", "*I'm not telling you to ape...*").
*   **Friendly Sign-off:** Closes articles with a warm sign-off and personal signature (*"Thanks for reading. 💙"* or *"Thank you for making it this far. - Miles"*). Emojis (typically blue hearts `💙` or smiles `😀`) are used at the end of sections or sign-offs to keep the tone friendly.

### 2. Spacing & Visual Formatting (Cohesive Spacing)
*   **Cohesive Paragraphs:** Group sentences into short, readable paragraph blocks of **1 to 3 sentences**, separated by double line breaks (`\n\n`).
*   **Heavy Use of Lists:** Break down steps, opportunities, or concepts using numbered (`1.`, `2.`) or bulleted (`*`) lists.
*   **Visual Highlights:** Always use bold headers inside lists for quick scanning (e.g. `* **[Item Name]**: [Detail]`).

### 3. Grammar & Pacing Flow (Dynamic Pacing)
*   **Standard Sentence Capitalization:** Write standard sentences starting with a capital letter and ending with a period. 
*   **Dynamic Pacing:** Avoid rigid sentence lengths. Mix short, punchy statements with longer compound sentences to explain technical mechanics.
*   **Active Verbs:** Write in the active present or past tense, focusing on direct actions and stripping passive verb padding.

### 4. Vocabulary & Niche Slang
*   **High-Energy Slang:** Uses highly engaging, informal trading and tech terminology to make the guide feel exclusive and conversational (*pure sauce, sleeping on, prints money, DCA'd, ape, normies*).
*   **Clear Analogies:** Demystify technical or financial jargon immediately on the spot using short, simple, everyday analogies (e.g., vector database = "memory drawer").

### 5. 🇮🇩 Indonesian-Specific Copywriting Rules (Apply ONLY for Indonesian Drafts)
*   **Indonesian Peer Pronouns:** Always use *"aku"* instead of *"saya"* and *"kamu"* instead of *"anda"* in database descriptions and narrative posts.
*   **Indonesian Verb Prefix Stripping:** Strip active prefixes (like *me-*, *meng-*, *mem-*) from verbs to keep sentences punchy and direct (e.g. write *"dia taruh"* instead of *"dia menaruh"*; *"pemerintah bangun"* instead of *"pemerintah membangun"*).
*   **Bilingual Jakarta Tech Slang:** Blend technical English terms (*bridge, LP, EVM, TVL, setup*) with casual local slang (*cuan, retail, REKT, ngeri, apesnya*).
*   **Abbreviation Shorthand:** Use *Korsel* (not Korea Selatan), *AS* or *US* (not Amerika Serikat), and *Ijin* (not izin).
*   **Word Reduction:** Strip filler or redundant words to keep sentences minimal but understandable (e.g., write *"kursi ini kuning"* instead of *"warna kursi ini adalah kuning"*).
*   **No "101" Synonyms:** Do not use the term "101" in translations. Use *dasar-dasar, fondasi, esensi, fundamental*.
*   **Conversational Analogies:** Explain technical terms on the spot using simple local analogies (e.g., explaining a fund as a *"kantong investasi"*).

## 🛑 Concrete Constraints (DOs & DONTs)

### 🟢 DOs
*   **DO** write standard capitalized sentences starting with capital letters and ending with periods.
*   **DO** write cohesive paragraphs of 1–3 sentences separated by double line breaks.
*   **DO** use active, high-energy verbs.
*   **DO** sign off friendly at the end of the article (e.g. using blue hearts `💙` and a signature).
*   **DO** apply the **Universal Binary Pronoun Rule** strictly:
    *   *🟢 Collective Human Roles (Traders, Consumers, Creators):* Use **We / Us / Our / Kita**.
    *   *🔴 External Entities (Protocols, Exchanges, Corporations):* Use **It / Its / They / Their / Ia / Mereka / -nya**.

### 🔴 DONTs
*   **DON'T** write single-sentence lines separated by commas and lowercase continuations.
*   **DON'T** use passive voice or long preamble setups.
*   **DON'T** use Indonesian words, slang, or grammar rules (like prefix stripping) in English drafts.
*   **DON'T** create dense paragraph blocks without double line spacing.
