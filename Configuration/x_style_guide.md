# Style Guide: Copywriting and Style Analysis for X (Twitter)

This guide provides the exact rules, voice parameters, structural guidelines, and formatting requirements to replicate the writer's style for X (Twitter) posts.

---

## 1. Voice and Tone
The voice is that of a **pragmatic macroeconomist and tech analyst**. It is analytical yet highly conversational, delivering deep business insights in a punchy, easily digestible format without artificial hype or filler words.

*   **Objective & Realist:** Avoids moralizing or emotional bias. Analyzes events through the lenses of geopolitics, capital flow, business margins, and long-term incentives.
*   **Counter-Intuitive:** Pierces the mainstream narrative. Looks for the hidden secondary effects (e.g., why a tiny 0.1% forecast drop actually triggers massive layoffs).
*   **Casual yet Authoritative:** Writes like a knowledgeable trading peer talking to other traders/investors. Uses direct address ("Kamu Gamer?", "bagi kita retail").
*   **Dry & Playful Ending:** Closes with a custom-written, contextually relevant dry observation or a cynical question to spark engagement. Do not copy-paste example catchphrases verbatim; write a unique ending tailored to the specific post's context, maintaining the same dry, cynical tone (e.g. asking an ironic question about the specific asset or referencing the specific losers of the trade).

## 3. Formatting & Visual Spacing
Visual layout is critical for readability on mobile screens. The style relies on high breathing room and short text blocks.

*   **Double Line Breaks (1 Sentence Per Block):** Every single sentence, bullet point, or logical transition must be separated by a double line break (`\n\n`). Never group multiple sentences into standard paragraphs.
*   **Max 33 Words Per Sentence:** Write complete, natural sentences in standard sentence case (max **33 words** per sentence). Use standard punctuation (periods `.`, colons `:`, dashes `-`) at sentence ends. Do NOT artificially chop single sentences into multiple 8-word line fragments using commas and lowercase letters.
*   **List Formatting:**
    *   Use numbered lists (`1.`, `2.`, `3.`) or lettered lists (`a.`, `b.`) to break down complex timelines or multi-factor issues.
    *   Use bold headers for list items to allow quick scanning (e.g., `1. Wawancara 'Show and Tell'`).
    *   Use simple dashes (`-`) for clean attribute breakdowns.
*   **Emojis:** Used very sparingly. Only place a single emoji (typically `😀` or a dry smile) at the very end of a post or inside direct replies to emphasize irony.

## 4. Narrative Structures & Templates

### The Historical Benchmark & Breaking News Template (Draft 1 Sub-Drafts)
Designed for ultra-fast, high-density breaking news alerts anchored by historical perspective.

* **Length Limit**: Strictly **60 words maximum per sub-draft**.
* **Pacing**: 1 sentence per block separated by double line breaks (`\n\n`) (up to 33 words max per sentence).
* **Step 1: Current Event (WHAT & WHEN)**:
  * Open with a bold, high-impact headline.
  * Present ONLY the primary **WHAT** (core event) and **WHEN** (key date, timeframe, or primary metric).
* **Step 2: Historical / Benchmark Anchor Categories**:
  * **Category A (Historical Event Anchor)**: Cites the last time a similar market, policy, or historical milestone occurred and its quantitative outcome.
  * **Category B (Parallel Industry Playbook)**: Cites a past industry playbook, competitive strategy, or adoption volume benchmark.
  * **Category C (Entity Track-Record Anchor)**: Cites a past decision, statement, threat, or policy made by the same executive, company, or government entity and its eventual result.
* **Fact-First & Comparative Question**:
  * Close with a sharp comparative question or observation contrasting the historical benchmark against today's event. End cleanly without filler catchphrases.

### The Standard Breaking News & Short Insight Template (Draft 2 Standard)
Designed for ultra-fast, high-density breaking news alerts without historical anchors.

* **Length Limit**: Strictly **60 words maximum**.
* **Structure**: A bold headline followed by a compact narrative flow.
* **Step 1: Ruthless Fact Filtering (WHAT & WHEN)**:
  * Open with a bold, high-impact headline. Present ONLY the primary **WHAT** (the core event) and **WHEN** (the key date, timeframe, or primary metric).
  * Avoid listing secondary details, minor sub-points, or extended background context.
* **Step 2: Smart Insight Selection (WHY, HOW, or WHO)**:
  * Dynamically evaluate the research brief and select the **SINGLE most valuable aspect** from:
    - **WHY** (e.g. *Why is this happening?*)
    - **HOW** (e.g. *How does this work or unfold?*)
    - **WHO** (e.g. *Who is most affected or gains/loses?*)
  * Deliver that core insight directly in 1–2 concise narrative lines.
* **Fact-First Principle**:
  * Prioritize concrete, high-utility facts and insights over filler text or forced catchphrases. End cleanly on the insight itself.

### Headline Formation
Start with a bold, high-impact title that summarizes the core conflict, mega-number, or crisis.
*   *Format:* `[Subject] + [High Stakes Action/Crisis/Big Number]`
*   *Examples:* 
    *   "Korsel invest 1 Quadrilion Won ($750 Miliar) di AI"
    *   "OpenAI Busuk Sampai Akar"
    *   "Volkswagen Dalam Masalah Besar"

### The Rhetorical Question-and-Answer Flow (News-First Style)
Establish authority and credibility first by stating the core news and concrete figures upfront, before transitioning into the Q&A format.

*   **Structure:**
    1. **Sentence 1 (Headline):** Bold news announcement.
    2. **Sentence 2 (Fact/Valuation):** Immediate quantitative data point to back up the headline.
    3. **Sentence 3 (Transition Question):** A rhetorical question to kick off the contextual explanation.
    4. **Subsequent lines:** Alternating Q&A format guides the user through the macro context.

*   *Example:* 
    > Saham Microstrategy diborong Wallstreet
    > 
    > Capital Group borong saham MicroStrategy lewat berbagai fund.
    > 
    > Fund ETF pegang $161 juta, fund ANCFX pegang $1.78 Milyar.
    > 
    > Kenapa mereka borong saham ini?
    > 
    > Untuk dapat eksposur Bitcoin secara legal.

### The Geopolitical / Macro Pivot
Contrast a seemingly simple action with its massive long-term or global impact.
*   *Example:* 
    > Apakah Pemerintah Korsel dapat saham di kawasan baru ini?
    > 
    > Tidak
    > 
    > Lalu untuk apa repot repot dukung kawasan industri ini?
    > 
    > Mereka berpikir untuk masa depan, bukan jangka pendek.

### The "Context" Section
For complex macro topics, add a small, isolated context section at the bottom to break down the scale of the numbers.
*   *Example:*
    > Context:
    > Total GDP global saat ini adalah $100 triliun.
    > 
    > 0.1% dari GPD global = $100 Milyar

## 5. Concrete Rules (DOs & DONTs)

### DOs
*   **DO** start with a strong, capitalized hook.
*   **DO** use double line breaks (`\n\n`) between *every* sentence block.
*   **DO** keep sentences short, clear, and active (max **33 words** per sentence).
*   **DO** explain the direct flow of money (how a macro decision affects "kita retail").
*   **DO** use English business/tech terms directly if they are industry standard.
*   **DO** use clean lists with bold headers to summarize events or statistics.
*   **DO** strip active prefixes (*me-*, *meng-*) and use Indonesian root verbs (*kata dasar*) for a punchier, raw tone.
*   **DO** remove all non-essential filler words to simplify sentence structure.
*   **DO** use **"Show, Don't Tell" step-by-step flow** to prove scale or danger. State the base event, present the raw quantity, and then provide a relative comparison to a known benchmark so the reader realizes the scale/danger themselves.
*   **DO** bridge logical gaps between sentences. If you state an action or event that raises an immediate question (e.g., "Why did they buy through multiple funds instead of one?"), you must explain the reason in the very next sentence.
*   **DO** simplify and explain specialized jargon (e.g., "fund," "ETF," "AUM," "arbitrage") immediately when first introduced. Use short, simple, everyday language (e.g., describing a fund as a "kantong investasi" or professional investment pool) before listing numbers.
*   **DO** identify the party most affected by the event (e.g., pension funds, insurance companies, commercial banks, tech giants, foreign governments) and focus the narrative takeaway on them. 
*   **DO** preserve the **"Red Thread" (Logical Core)**: If your post hinges on a seemingly small change or figure (e.g., a 0.36% yield difference), you must anticipate the reader's natural skepticism (e.g., *"why does 0.36% matter?"*) and explicitly explain the mechanism/math (e.g., long duration + massive institutional scale = huge absolute dollar impact) so the logic remains sound.
*   **DO** provide comparative context for every metric or statistic. Never state a number in isolation. Compare the current figure to its **prior period, benchmark, forecast, or expectation**, showing both the delta and the reference value (e.g., *"GDP kuartal dua hanya tumbuh 4.3%, meleset 0.2% dari GDP kuartal 1 sebelumnya yaitu 4.5%"* or *"investasi properti hancur minus 18% di semester pertama, anjlok dari ekspektasi awal yaitu minus 12%"*).
*   **DO** filter out non-essential details, names, acronyms, or secondary data points if they do not directly reinforce the core premise of the post. Keep the writing focused only on the main entities and the logical flow of the narrative.

### DONTs
*   **DON'T** write dense text blocks. If two lines of text are joined without a blank line, fix it.
*   **DON'T** use overly academic Indonesian (e.g., write *cuan* instead of *keuntungan*, *retail* instead of *investor ritel*).
*   **DON'T** use passive voice or long preamble setups. Get straight to the point.
*   **DON'T** use complex verb structures or filler descriptions (e.g., avoid "warna kursi ini kuning", write "kursi ini kuning").
*   **DON'T** flood the post with emojis, hashtags, or handles. Keep the visual interface clean.
*   **DON'T** preach or make moral judgments. Focus on the raw incentives.
*   **DON'T** present isolated statistics or percentages without comparative context (e.g., avoid stating *"GDP tumbuh 4.3%"* without showing how it relates to the previous period, expectations, or forecasts).
*   **DON'T** use lazy adjective labels or hype words (e.g., "raksasa", "masif", "huge", "sangat besar", "sangat bahaya") to declare a conclusion.
*   **DON'T** introduce technical terms or complex actions without explanation. Never assume the reader knows financial acronyms or why institutional players structured a trade in a certain way.
*   **DON'T** default to retail ("kita retail" or "Retail REKT") as the victim or target audience unless they are indeed the group impacted the most by the news.

## 7. Glossary Post Style & Structure

### Voice, Tone & Grammar
* **Linguistic Style (Cynical Smart Peer)**: Blend your signature smart, cynical, dry, and witty copywriting style with a slightly more structured/formal tone. The post must feel like a smart, friendly peer explaining complex concepts directly, not an academic textbook.
* **Indonesian Pronouns (Aku/Kamu)**: You MUST use "aku" instead of "saya" and "kamu" instead of "anda" in all Indonesian database entries and Indonesian narrative X posts to maintain a close, peer-to-peer relationship.
* **The Universal Binary Pronoun Rule**: When drafting any database description, analogy (ELI10/Bahasa Bayi), or narrative post, evaluate the "actor" of the action and apply pronouns strictly based on this question:
  > **"Does this actor represent a collective human identity that the reader and writer naturally belong to in the context of the post?"**
  * **🟢 YES: Use "We / Us / Our" (Indonesian: "kita" / "aku" / "kamu")**:
    * If the actor is a group that represents the reader/writer's collective human role in that scenario.
    * *In Trading (Traders/Retail)*: "A margin call hits **our** accounts / akun **kita**."
    * *In Macroeconomics (Consumers/Common People/Citizens)*: "Inflation squeezes **our** grocery budgets / belanja **kita**."
    * *In Taxation (Taxpayers/Citizens)*: "The government raises **our** income tax / pajak **kita**."
    * *In Business/Entrepreneurship (Business Owners)*: "If **our** margins compress, **we** must cut costs / **kita** wajib pangkas biaya."
  * **🔴 NO: Use Specific Noun + "It / Its / They / Their" (Indonesian: Specific Noun + "ia" / "mereka" / Suffix "-nya")**:
    * If the actor is an external institution, code system, smart contract, corporation, or distinct organization that the reader/writer does not belong to.
    * *Blockchain Mechanics (Protocols/Smart Contracts)*: "**The protocol** owns **its** own liquidity pool / **Protokol** memiliki liquidity pool-**nya** sendiri."
    * *Central Banks (Sovereigns)*: "**The Fed** prints currency to expand **its** balance sheet / **The Fed** mencetak mata uang untuk memperluas balance sheet-**nya**."
    * *Corporations (Companies)*: "**MicroStrategy** buys Bitcoin for **its** treasury / **MicroStrategy** membeli Bitcoin untuk kas-**nya**."
    * *Commercial Banks (Banks)*: "**JPMorgan** increases **its** reserves / **JPMorgan** meningkatkan cadangan-**nya**."
    * *Security Exploits (Hackers)*: "**The hacker** drains the vault to **their** wallet / **Hacker** menguras dana ke dompet **mereka**."
* **Vocabulary (Conversational over Stiff)**: Use direct, conversational trading verbs and nouns. For example:
  * **Indonesian**: Use *broker* (not *perusahaan pialang*), *pakai* (not *menggunakan*), *kena* (not *menghadapi/terkena*), *wajib* (not *memaksa*), *kasih* (not *memberikan*), *minta* (not *menuntut*), and *Ketika* (not *Selama*).
  * **English**: Use *use* (not *utilize*), *have to* or *must* (not *are required to/are forced to*), *get hit with* or *trigger* (not *encounter/experience*), *give* (not *provide/furnish*), *when* (not *during/in the course of*), and *but they are still* (not *Although... they remain*).
* **Dynamic Prefix Stripping**: While you should maintain full sentence structure, you MUST strip prefixes from action verbs in Indonesian (e.g., use *tiup*, *setor*, *beli*, *jual*, *tutupi*, *koreksi*) when explaining market dynamics to keep the speed fast and natural.
* **No Meta-Transitions or Importance Labels**: Do not write sentences declaring why a concept is important (e.g., do NOT write *"X is important because..."* or *"This concept is vital because..."*). Also, do NOT write roadmap sentences explaining the post structure (e.g., do NOT write *"This leads us to our next concept"*). Instead, transition by showing the immediate downstream financial consequence or pain (e.g., *"...mereka berisiko rugi karena margin call"* in Indonesian; or *"...leaving us vulnerable to a margin call"* in English).
* **Spacing**: Maintain cohesive paragraphs (cohesive multi-sentence paragraph blocks). Do NOT use the 1-line-per-sentence spacing approach of standard event posts.
* **Bilingual Requirement**: Every term must be drafted simultaneously in English and Indonesian following the respective templates below.

### The 2-Term X Post Structure (Clean Narrative)
When outputting the final post, do NOT use Markdown headers (`##`) or bold section labels (`**What it is?**`). The text must flow as clean paragraphs, structured exactly as:

1. **The 3-Line Launchpad**:
   * **Line 1: Event Headline Hook**: An event-focused, high-impact headline directly reporting the news event (e.g., "MegaETH Just Shut Down Its Flagship Accelerator" or "US CPI Release Just Scared The Market").
   * **Line 2: Event Context/Fact**: A 1-sentence summary reporting the quantitative facts, numbers, or direct impact of the event (e.g., "It rose 0.5% compared to last quarter, alerting market of a potential sticky inflation problem.").
   * **Line 3: Dynamic Blueprint Intro (with Expectation Management Tag)**: Introduce both terms, setting up what mechanism we will analyze, while explicitly using a beginner-friendly synonym (e.g., *basics, foundation, essentials, fundamentals, building blocks* in English; or *dasar-dasar, fondasi, esensi, fundamental* in Indonesian).
     * *Example English*: "In this post, we will touch the basics of Value Capture and Ecosystem Attrition to understand how they form a loop that stalls protocol growth."
     * *Example Indonesian*: "Kali ini, kita akan menyentuh dasar-dasar dari Value Capture and Ecosystem Attrition untuk memahami bagaimana mereka membentuk loop yang menghambat pertumbuhan protokol."
2. **Term 1**: 4 paragraphs matching the What, Why, ELI10, and Example sections (without the labels, using cohesive paragraphs).
3. **Transition Connector**: A short paragraph bridging Term 1 ➔ Term 2. You MUST explicitly write out the actual word of Term 2 near the end of this paragraph to establish a natural logical connection. Do not use placeholders.
4. **Term 2**: 4 paragraphs matching the What, Why, ELI10, and Example sections (without the labels, using cohesive paragraphs).
5. **Conclusion**: A summary paragraph of the 2-term pipeline/loop for an "aha!" moment (no hashtags or follow requests at the end, just end cleanly on your dry, cynical final observation).
